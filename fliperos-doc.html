FliperOS 0.10

English | Português

Ubuntu 24.04 (amd64) for arcade cabinets with a CRT monitor, in the style of GroovyArcade: a 15 kHz kernel, Switchres, emulators and frontends ready to play, and a setup menu (fliperos-setup) made for the tube's screen.

In development: 0.10 is tested in QEMU and on a 15 kHz CRT cabinet. Full documentation in the wiki. Website: fliperos.juniorter.in (source in website/).

What's included

  • Video: 6.18 LTS kernel with the 15 kHz patches, boot at 15, 25 or 31 kHz or LCD, Switchres.
  • Emulators: GroovyMAME, RetroArch, Flycast, PCSX2, Dolphin, Supermodel, SM2-Emu (Model 2), Hypseus Singe and OpenBOR; Wine, Steam and Heroic through Setup > System > Extras.
  • Frontends: Attract-Mode Plus, EmulationStation (ES-DE), Pegasus and Fightcade 2, whose online matches play in each game's native resolution on the tube.
  • Light gun: GunCon 2 out of the box (driver via DKMS), calibrated for the tube in Setup > Joysticks. Wheels too.
  • Setup: video, geometry, audio, network, scraper, ROM lists, MAME romset cleaning, controls and latency.
  • Folders roms, bios, media and config in ~ and over the network (Samba); LXDE desktop with an app store.
  • SSH on from the first boot (ssh fliperos@fliperos.local, password fliperos). Under the menus it's a regular Ubuntu, so an AI assistant can configure it over SSH.

From zero to playing: step by step

This guide goes from an empty cabinet to a game on screen. Each step says what you will see and what to press. The details behind each screen are in the wiki pages linked along the way.

1. What you need

  • A PC (amd64) for the cabinet. For arcade games up to the 90s and consoles up to the PlayStation, a 2-core CPU, 4 GB of RAM and a 16 GB disk are enough. For PS2, Model 3 and Dreamcast, a 4-core CPU with AVX2 (Core i7-7700, Ryzen 5 3600 or newer), 16 GB and an SSD. The disk you install to is erased completely.
  • A graphics card with analog output. The best fit is an AMD Radeon with VGA or DVI-I from the HD 2000 series to the R7/R9 (GCN 1.0), which generates 15 kHz natively. Intel and NVIDIA work at 15 kHz with "super resolution" modes. Cards without an analog output need a DisplayPort/HDMI to VGA converter.
  • The monitor: an arcade CRT (15 kHz), a TV through a VGA to SCART/component adapter, or a 25/31 kHz monitor. An LCD also works.
  • A USB stick of 4 GB or more (it will be erased).
  • A USB keyboard for the installation. After that, the panel or a gamepad moves through all the menus.
  • A network connection (cable or Wi-Fi), to copy games from another computer and to receive updates. Optional, but it makes everything easier.
  • Another computer (Windows, Linux or macOS) to write the USB stick and, later, to copy the games over the network.

2. Download the ISO

  1. Open Releases and download fliperos-0.10.iso from the latest version (what changed is in CHANGELOG.md).
  2. If the release lists a SHA-256, compare it: sha256sum fliperos-0.10.iso on Linux, Get-FileHash fliperos-0.10.iso in Windows PowerShell.

3. Write the USB stick

The ISO must be written byte for byte. Programs that rebuild the stick's boot structure break it: Rufus in its default mode ("ISO image"), Ventoy and UNetbootin don't work.

  • Windows, macOS or Linux, the easiest: balenaEtcher. Flash from file > choose the ISO > Select target > the USB stick > Flash!.
  • Windows with Rufus: choose the stick and the ISO, click START and, when it asks, choose "Write in DD Image mode".
  • Linux: find the stick with lsblk (here /dev/sdX; the wrong device erases the wrong disk) and run:

bash sudo dd if=fliperos-0.10.iso of=/dev/sdX bs=4M status=progress conv=fsync

4. Prepare the cabinet's BIOS

Enter the BIOS/UEFI setup (usually Del, F2 or F10 right after power-on) and:

  1. Disable Secure Boot: the 15 kHz kernel is custom and unsigned, and with Secure Boot on it doesn't start.
  2. SATA mode on AHCI (not RAID or Intel RST); otherwise the installer doesn't see the disk.
  3. Boot from the USB stick: put it first in the boot order, or use the one-time boot menu (often F8, F11 or F12). BIOS (legacy) and UEFI both work.

Save and exit with the stick plugged in.

5. Boot from the USB stick

  1. Wait about 30 seconds without pressing anything. The boot menu (15 kHz, 25 kHz, 31 kHz, LCD...) is drawn at 31 kHz, so it doesn't show on a 15 kHz tube. With no key pressed, the 15 kHz entry boots by itself, which is the safe one for a cabinet. On a PC monitor or an LCD, you can pick SVGA / LCD monitor in the menu instead.
  2. The FliperOS splash appears, already at 15 kHz.
  3. Output test. FliperOS turns on one video output at a time and a voice says "Testing output VGA 1. If you can see this screen, press Enter." When the text "If you can see this screen clearly, press ENTER" is readable on your monitor, press Enter. Without Enter it moves on to the next output and repeats the round up to three times.
  4. Testing Results shows the card, the output, the mode and the frequency. Choose Validate settings (or Repeat test).
  5. Monitor type: choose your monitor from the list (for example generic_15 for a common arcade monitor or TV, arcade_15, ntsc, pal, or a specific model). The list opens on the suggestion for the boot entry. The full table of boot entries is in Install.

6. Install to the disk

The FliperOS Setup menu opens (Install to HD/SSD, Recovery Mode, Terminal, Shutdown). The arrows move, Enter confirms and Esc goes back.

  1. Choose Install to HD/SSD.
  2. Do you want to configure video? Choose Yes to adjust it now (you can also do it later in Setup > Video Setup):
  3. Monitor Type: the same monitor as in the test;
  4. Monitor Orientation: horizontal, or vertical for a rotated monitor (vertical games);
  5. Resolution: the modes your monitor and card accept (the suggestion is fine);
  6. Geometry: a grid to center the picture and fit its size on the tube. Use the arrows to adjust and Enter to save.
  7. Automatically Partition: choose the disk by model, size and type (USB/NVMe/SATA). If yours isn't listed, My drive is not listed (details) explains why (usually the SATA mode, step 4).
  8. WARNING: it shows the disk that will be completely erased. Check it and confirm (No goes back to the list).
  9. Installing FliperOS: a progress bar with the current step. It takes a few minutes.
  10. Installation completed successfully: remove the USB stick and choose Reboot now.

7. First boot of the installed system

  1. The cabinet boots straight to the splash, without text.
  2. Choose default launcher: what opens every time the cabinet is turned on. For a cabinet, choose Attract-Mode Plus (a game list with artwork, made for arcade). The others are EmulationStation (ES-DE), Pegasus, RetroArch, the LXDE desktop or the FliperOS Setup itself. You can change it later in Setup > Games > Frontend.
  3. When you leave the launcher (Esc), the FliperOS menu appears: Start frontend, Setup, Start desktop, Exit to shell, Shutdown / Reboot. The title shows the cabinet's IP address. Every item is explained in The menus, item by item.
  4. Network: a network cable works by itself. For Wi-Fi: Setup > Network Setup, choose the network and type the password. Once connected, the IP appears in the menu title.
  5. Updates: at each boot with internet, if there is a FliperOS update, a screen describes it and asks Apply now or Not now.

8. Set up the controls

In the menus, the panel or a gamepad already acts as the keyboard: directions = arrows, button 1 = Enter, button 2 = Esc.

  1. Setup > Joysticks > Button mapping.
  2. For each player, press what it asks for, in order: up, down, left, right, buttons 1 to 6 in panel order (1 2 3 in the top row, 4 5 6 in the bottom row), Start and Coin. The first input pressed tells which controller belongs to that player. With nothing pressed for 8 seconds, that input is skipped.
  3. The mapping goes to every emulator at once (GroovyMAME, RetroArch, Flycast, PCSX2, Dolphin, Supermodel, Hypseus and OpenBOR).
  4. Light gun (GunCon 2), wheel and pedals have their own calibrators in the same menu, and Setup > System > Quirks fixes USB devices that arrive wrong (for example a dual encoder showing up as a single player). See Controls.

9. Copy your games

Use only games you have the right to use (your own dumps, or the free homebrew from Setup > Games > Downloader > Free games).

Use the LXDE desktop. Plug a USB mouse and keyboard into the cabinet and choose Start desktop in the FliperOS menu. It is a regular desktop, and it makes all the work with games easier:

  • Folders: the file manager opens your home folder, with roms, bios, media and config. You see where each file goes and can create, rename and move files with the mouse.
  • Browser: Falkon (browser) in the desktop menu, to search for the torrents of your games and BIOS.
  • Torrents: Transmission (torrent) opens the .torrent files and magnet links from the browser. When the download finishes, move the files from Downloads to the right folder in roms.
  • External storage: plug in a USB stick or an external disk. It is mounted by itself and gets an icon on the desktop: open it, copy the files and paste them into the right folder in roms (or bios).
  • To go back to the games: Logout in the desktop menu > back to the FliperOS menu.

Over the network, from another computer on the same network:

  • Windows: in File Explorer's address bar type \\fliperos\roms (or \\IP-of-the-cabinet\roms) and press Enter. If it asks for a login: user fliperos, password fliperos.
  • macOS: Finder > Go > Connect to Server > smb://fliperos.local/roms.
  • Linux: smb://fliperos.local/roms in the file manager.
  • Also available: \\fliperos\bios (BIOS files), \\fliperos\media (artwork) and \\fliperos\config (emulator settings).

SFTP also works: fliperos@fliperos.local, password fliperos.

Each emulator has its folder in roms. Copy the files as they come: MAME sets stay zipped and are never renamed (sf2ce.zip, not Street Fighter II.zip).

Folder Games Emulator
mame Arcade: MAME sets (.zip, .7z) and their CHD folders GroovyMAME
naomi, naomi2, atomiswave Sega Naomi and Sammy Atomiswave sets Flycast
model3 Sega Model 3 sets Supermodel
model2 Sega Model 2 sets Model 2 (SM2-Emu, Games menu; built on first launch)
dreamcast .gdi, .cdi, .chd Flycast
ps2 .iso, .chd, .cso PCSX2
dolphin GameCube and Wii (.iso, .rvz, .wbfs) Dolphin
retroarch/<core> Consoles and computers; the _info.txt in each folder lists the extensions RetroArch

BIOS go in bios, never in a games folder: bios/mame for MAME (neogeo.zip, qsound.zip...), bios/dc for Flycast (naomi.zip, awbios.zip, dc_boot.bin), bios/ps2 for PCSX2, and the bios folder itself for the RetroArch cores.

Has a full MAME romset? Setup > Games > Cleaner > MAME ROM Cleaner keeps only the games that work and suit your cabinet (horizontal or vertical, the controls you have) and puts them in the right emulator's folder. More in ROMs, BIOS and artwork.

10. Make the frontend show the games

  1. Setup > Games > Frontend > choose Attract-Mode Plus again (even if it already is the default). The Setup creates a system for each folder that has games. Do this again every time a folder gets its first games.
  2. Real game names (Street Fighter II instead of sf2): Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List, for the arcade folders. It works offline and also brings year, manufacturer and the clones.
  3. Artwork (screenshots, logos, videos, descriptions): Setup > Games > Scraper, which needs internet. See Scraper.
  4. New games added later to a folder that already shows in Attract-Mode: run Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List or Setup > Games > Scraper again. Details in Adding games.

11. Play

  1. In the FliperOS menu choose Start frontend (or just turn the cabinet on: the default launcher opens by itself).
  2. In Attract-Mode Plus: left/right change the system (MAME, Naomi, PCSX2...) and up/down move through the games. Each system comes with filters (All Games, Most Played, genres, manufacturers, years); Tab opens Attract-Mode's configuration, where Controls binds buttons to change filters and anything else.
  3. Press button 1 (or Enter) on a game. The screen goes black for a moment while Switchres sets the video mode for that game, and the game starts.
  4. Insert a credit with Coin and start with Start.
  5. Leaving the game: in GroovyMAME, Esc on the keyboard. In RetroArch, Start + Coin together open its menu, then Quick Menu > Close Content. The other emulators use their own exit key or menu (Emulators). You go back to the game list.
  6. Esc in Attract-Mode (and confirming) leaves the frontend and returns to the FliperOS menu, where Shutdown / Reboot turns the cabinet off safely.
  7. The cabinet's power button (a short press): with a game open, it closes the game and returns to the game list; in the game list, it turns the cabinet off safely. So from inside a game it takes two presses to power off: wait for the list to show before the second one.

12. If something goes wrong

  • No picture at boot: wait the 30 seconds (the menu doesn't show at 15 kHz). On a 15 kHz-only tube, connect an LCD temporarily to choose another boot entry.
  • The installer doesn't list the disk: SATA mode on AHCI in the BIOS. My drive is not listed (details) shows what Linux sees.
  • The picture is off-center or too big: Setup > Video Setup > Geometry.
  • Two or three monitors (Darius, Punch-Out!!, F355 Challenge): Setup > Video Setup > Multiple Monitors, see Multiple monitors.
  • A system doesn't appear in the frontend: the folder needs at least one game file (the _info.txt doesn't count); then choose the frontend again in Setup > Games > Frontend.
  • A game doesn't open: turn on Setup > System > Debug mode to see the emulator's messages on screen; without it they are saved in /opt/fliperos/logs. Check the BIOS in bios.
  • No sound: Setup > Audio Setup chooses the card and the volume and plays a test.
  • Something the menus don't cover: over SSH (ssh fliperos@fliperos.local, password fliperos) it's a regular Ubuntu 24.04, and an AI assistant with terminal access can configure it for you. On a network you don't control, change the password with passwd.

The menus, item by item

Everything in the menus, in the order it appears on screen. In any menu: arrows move, Enter confirms, Esc goes back (or choose Return). The panel or a gamepad works too: directions = arrows, button 1 = Enter, button 2 = Esc, button 3 = Space (marks items in lists with checkboxes).

FliperOS Setup (on the USB stick)

The menu that opens when you boot from the USB stick, before installing.

  • Install to HD/SSD: installs FliperOS on a disk, as in step 6. It asks for the video settings, the disk (which will be completely erased) and shows the progress.
  • Recovery Mode: repairs a FliperOS already installed on this computer, without installing again. It looks for the installation and offers:
  • Open a terminal in the installed system: a command line inside the installed system, to fix something by hand;
  • Reinstall the bootloader: puts Limine back on the disk when the computer no longer boots FliperOS (after installing Windows on another disk, for example);
  • Apply this session's video settings (new video card): changed the video card or the monitor? Boot from the USB stick, adjust the video and bring those settings into the installed system;
  • Restore system files (keeps ROMs and settings): puts the original FliperOS files back, keeping your games, saves and settings.
  • Terminal: a command line on the USB stick itself.
  • Shutdown: power off or reboot.

FliperOS menu (installed system)

It appears when you leave the frontend (Esc). The title shows the cabinet's name, its IP address and how full the disk is.

  • Start frontend: opens the default launcher (Attract-Mode Plus, for example). When you leave it, you come back to this menu.
  • Setup: all the settings, explained below.
  • Start desktop: opens LXDE, a regular desktop with a mouse, file manager, browser (Falkon), torrent client (Transmission) and app store. It is the easiest way to organize folders and copy games (step 9). To leave: Logout in the desktop menu, which opens a small menu: back to the FliperOS menu, reboot, power off or cancel.
  • Exit to shell: a full-screen command line. Type fliperos-menu to return to the menu.
  • Shutdown / Reboot: Power off or Reboot, always asking first. Use it instead of switching the cabinet off at the wall. The cabinet's power button does the same without asking, and with a game open it only closes the game.

Setup

The items come in the order of setting up a cabinet: picture, sound, network, controls, games and the rest of the system. Under the list, a balloon says what the item under the cursor is for.

  • Video Setup: how the picture comes out on the monitor. The top of the screen shows what is set now.
  • Monitor Type: the kind of monitor (arcade CRT 15 kHz, 25 kHz, 31 kHz, TV, LCD...). It decides which video modes are allowed;
  • Monitor Orientation: horizontal, or vertical (rotated clockwise or counterclockwise) for vertical games;
  • Resolution: the resolution of the menus and the frontend;
  • Geometry: a grid to center the picture and fit its size on the tube; the arrows adjust and Enter saves;
  • Reset Geometry: goes back to the monitor's default geometry;
  • Multiple Monitors: a second and a third arcade monitor, for games with 2 or 3 screens (Darius, Punch-Out!!, F355 Challenge). Inside: Monitor 2 and Monitor 3 (which video output each one uses), Multi-Screen Games (one screen per monitor, or everything on monitor 1), Game Screen Order (which monitor shows each screen) and Identify Monitors (turns each monitor off for 3 seconds, in order). See Multiple monitors.

Install

From the written media to the system on disk: writing the ISO, the boot menu, the video output test and the installation through FliperOS Setup.

Writing the media

Write the ISO byte for byte to a USB stick and disable Secure Boot (the 15 kHz kernel is custom and unsigned).

The ISO uses the Limine bootloader and is hybrid (xorriso + limine bios-install): the same image boots as a CD and as a USB stick, on BIOS and on UEFI. Writers that rebuild the boot structure break it — Rufus in its default mode ("ISO image"), Ventoy and UNetbootin don't work. Use balenaEtcher, or Rufus in "DD Image mode", or dd:

sudo dd if=fliperos-0.10.iso of=/dev/sdX bs=4M status=progress conv=fsync

Check /dev/sdX with lsblk first: the wrong device erases the wrong disk.

Boot menu

The same options as GroovyArcade (os/groovyarcade/syslinux/syslinux.cfg). Here you only choose the range Linux boots in; the output and the monitor are decided later, by the output test.

Entry Parameters For
15 kHz (default) video=640x480iS Arcade monitor or TV, 640x480 interlaced
25 kHz video=512x384S Medium resolution monitor
31 kHz video=640x480S High resolution monitor
SVGA / LCD monitor — PC monitor or LCD (its EDID applies)
Intel 15 kHz video=1280x480iS i915.no_ytiled_scanout=1 Intel: super resolution; the parameter comes from patch 09 and enables interlacing on Gen9
NVIDIA 15 kHz video=1280x480iS NVIDIA: super resolution
NTSC video=720x480iS NTSC TV
PAL video=768x576iS PAL TV
EDID progressive drm.edid_firmware=edid/generic_15_super_resp.bin video=e Switchres EDID, all outputs forced
EDID interlaced drm.edid_firmware=edid/generic_15_super_resi.bin video=e Same, interlaced

The S at the end of the mode comes from the 15 kHz patch: it uses the kernel's fixed table of low-dotclock modes (drm_modes_low_dotclock.c), and i means interlaced. Every entry carries quiet splash consoleblank=0 and radeon on SI/CIK cards.

30-second timer. With no key pressed, the 15 kHz entry boots, which is the safe one for a cabinet: an LCD receiving 15 kHz only shows "out of range", but 31 kHz on a 15 kHz-only tube can damage it.

The menu itself doesn't show on a 15 kHz CRT — it is drawn in the firmware's text mode (~31 kHz), before the kernel. GroovyArcade is the same. To choose another entry on a 15 kHz-only cabinet, connect an LCD temporarily; with the CRT, wait the 30 s. The first readable thing on the tube is the splash, already in the boot mode.

Output test and Testing Results

Ported from gatools (video/video.sh): as soon as the media boots, fliperos-setup:

  1. forces the analog outputs on ("Lights on") and explains the test, speaking through espeak-ng;
  2. turns all outputs off and enables one at a time, writing on/off/detect to /sys/class/drm/cardN-CONNECTOR/status (an analog output with no monitor detected is forced; a digital one with nothing connected is skipped);
  3. on each one, says "Testing output V G A 1. If you can see this screen, press Enter." and shows "If you can see this screen clearly, press ENTER." for 10 s;
  4. stops at the first ENTER and goes to the Testing Results screen (gatools tests all of them and picks the best; here, whoever is looking at the screen has already decided). Without ENTER, it repeats the round up to three times.

With two cards, the console is moved to the framebuffer of the card being tested (con2fbmap), as in gatools.

The Testing Results screen shows the GPU, driver, connector, current mode and horizontal frequency (read from the active CRTC by fliperos-video-check), whether the card generates low dotclocks, whether there is an interlaced mode, the EDID, whether the monitor was identified and whether the output will be forced at boot — with the same sentences as gatools' inform_user — and offers Validate settings or Repeat test.

Once validated, gatools' configure_from_connector applies:

  • Switchres EDID (dongle) → the monitor comes from the EDID itself;
  • otherwise, the monitor type is mandatory (the list opens on the suggestion of the boot entry);
  • the kernel line comes from the monitor: video=VGA-1:640x480iSe (the e when the output had to be forced); a card without low dotclock (Intel, NVIDIA) → super resolution 1280x480iS and dotclock_min 25.0 in Switchres; APU → interlace_force_even 1; EDID boot → drm.edid_firmware=CONNECTOR:edid/<monitor>.bin; LCD → nothing (its EDID applies).

Everything is saved in /etc/fliperos/fliperos.conf (GPU, driver, connector, detection, monitor, range, boot mode, kernel parameters, orientation, launcher), the equivalent of ga.conf, and in switchres.ini/mame.ini.

FliperOS Setup

The media's menu (gasetup's isomainmenu): Install to HD/SSD, Recovery Mode, Terminal and Shutdown. The title shows the host and the IP.

Install to HD/SSD

  1. Do you want to configure video? — Yes opens Video Setup:
  2. Monitor Type: the 29 Switchres presets, in the gatools list, plus Custom: your own crt_range0 (the timings that really fit your monitor), made on the Geometry grid or typed in (16 values, as in switchres.ini, of the same 15/25/31 kHz band as the preset). It sits on top of the preset that was in use, which still gives the boot resolution, the EDID and the Xorg range; choosing a preset again discards it;
  3. Monitor Orientation: horizontal, vertical clockwise, vertical counter-clockwise (console rotated with fbcon=rotate, panel_orientation in video=, MAME's ror/rol and the desktop through xrandr);
  4. Resolution: only the modes of the kernel table that the monitor's range and the card accept (without low dotclock, only the super resolutions), plus a custom resolution: tested on the Switchres grid and turned into an EDID (switchres -e), like worker_custom_video_mode;
  5. Geometry: the same geometry as GroovyArcade — Switchres' geometry.py, which draws the grid and returns the crt_range, saved as monitor custom in /etc/switchres.ini (the Monitor Type becomes Custom). The grid is drawn with the lines and refresh rate of the resolution chosen above (50 Hz in PAL modes; with no choice, 640x480), 8 pixels wider, like GroovyArcade's 648x480: at the exact boot mode, Switchres would have to replace the mode on screen each time an arrow reopens the grid, and the tool stopped at the first arrow. Downwards the image only moves while there are blank lines below it (Switchres doesn't go past the front porch); when it stops, the adjustment is the monitor's V-POS;
  6. Multiple Monitors: a second arcade monitor on another analog output (VGA, DVI-I) of the same card, and a third, or one on a second video card or on a digital output through an active VGA converter, as an experimental option, for games with 2 or 3 screens (Darius, The Ninja Warriors and Punch-Out!! in GroovyMAME, F355 Challenge in Flycast), with a switch to turn the multi-screen mode off. See Multiple monitors.
  7. Automatically Partition — lists the disks by model, size, path and bus (USB/NVMe/SATA). Not listed: the installation media itself, disks under 16 GiB, mounted or in swap, with RAID/LVM/encryption or active dependents — each one with the reason, above the list. My drive is not listed (details) shows everything Linux sees (lsblk, the controllers and the kernel messages): an HD/SSD that doesn't even show there is hidden before Linux, almost always by the BIOS SATA mode set to RAID/Intel RST (switch to AHCI) or by Intel VMD.
  8. WARNING — model, path and size of the disk that will be erased; No goes back to the disk choice. If the disk already has FliperOS, the warning suggests Recovery Mode.
  9. Installing FliperOS — a single screen, with the target disk, a real progress bar (the system copy comes from the unsquashfs percentage, like gasetup's dialog --gauge), the step and short messages. Command output goes to /var/log/fliperos-setup.log. On error: step, error, View log / Retry / Return.
  10. Installation completed successfully. Remove the installation CD/DVD/USB. — and Reboot now?

Partitioning: GPT, a 1 MiB BIOS boot partition, a 1 GiB FAT32 ESP, the rest in ext4. The new partitions have their old signatures wiped and the BIOS boot one is zeroed (a leftover file system from the previous disk there made limine bios-install refuse; it also runs with --force). Limine is installed for BIOS (stage 2 in the BIOS boot partition) and for UEFI (EFI/BOOT/BOOTX64.EFI, the removable path), without touching the NVRAM. Limine only reads FAT, so the kernel and initrd live on the ESP (/boot/efi/fliperos/), maintained by fliperos-limine-update — the equivalent of update-grub, called by the kernel and initramfs hooks. The kernel parameters are in /etc/default/fliperos-boot.

The new disk carries what was decided on the media: fliperos.conf, Switchres, MAME, Xorg, the kernel line, the configured Wi-Fi networks and the sound card.

Recovery Mode

Looks for disks with FliperOS (a FliperOS partition with the /etc/fliperos/installed marker) and offers: a terminal inside the installed system (chroot, gasetup's rescue_mode), reinstall the bootloader (the "Fix UEFI boot", for BIOS and UEFI), apply this session's video settings (graphics card replaced) and restore the system files from the media, keeping ROMs, saves, home, network, audio and video.

Installed system

What happens from boot onwards on an installed FliperOS: the default launcher, the Setup menu and what each of its items does. The desktop is covered in Desktop and theme.

Boot without text: from Limine straight to Plymouth, and the splash stays on screen until the menu or the frontend opens: fliperos-tty1 takes it down right before the launcher, the menu, the first boot or the update notice, so the login and the wait for the network (for the update check) don't leave the screen black. Ubuntu's plymouth-quit only takes it down after 90 seconds, as a safety net, and plymouth-quit-wait doesn't hold the tty1 login. The Limine menu is hidden (quiet: yes; a key during its 3 seconds shows it, with the diagnostics entry), the kernel and udev stay quiet (quiet loglevel=3 rd.udev.log_level=3 udev.log_level=3), the console cursor disappears (vt.global_cursor_default=0, and comes back in the shell), the automatic login writes nothing (agetty --skip-login --noissue, ~/.hushlogin) and the console stays quiet after boot (kernel.printk = 3 4 1 3 in /etc/sysctl.d/99-fliperos-console.conf: Ubuntu's 10-console-messages.conf set the level back to 4, and a kernel error after Plymouth, such as the one from the unused Intel HDMI codec, showed up on screen).

tty1 logs in by itself and follows the flow of GroovyArcade's .bash_profile (config/fliperos-tty1, called by ~/.zprofile): it opens the default launcher and, when it closes, the FliperOS menu. The menu is a loop: Start frontend and Start desktop make the setup exit and fliperos-tty1 opens the launcher on tty1 itself, as at boot, and returns to the menu when it closes. The setup runs as root inside sudo's pseudo-terminal; opened from there, X doesn't have tty1 as its terminal and can't switch VT (xf86OpenConsole: VT_ACTIVATE failed) — that was the cabinet's "desktop doesn't start". If X crashes on startup, the errors from its log stay on screen until Enter (a copy goes to /opt/fliperos/logs/Xorg-failed.log). The user's shell is zsh with Oh My Zsh and the Dracula theme (Desktop and theme); ~/.bash_profile calls the same flow, for whoever goes back to bash.

Controller in the menus: with the setup menu on screen, the controller acts as the keyboard — D-pad, hat or analog stick = arrows (repeat while held), button 1 / A / Start = Enter, button 2 / B / Select = Esc, button 3 / X / Y = Space (toggles in checklists), L / R = Page Up / Page Down. It works with arcade encoders (digital axis 0–255), gamepads and USB joysticks; light guns are left out (aiming would move through the menu). The fliperos-padkeys service (pure Python, the kernel's uinput) only translates while /run/fliperos/padkeys exists, which the setup creates when it opens and deletes when it exits: frontends, emulators and the desktop read the controller by themselves, without a button counting twice.

First boot: Choose default launcher, with the generated list of the launchers actually installed (Attract-Mode Plus, RetroArch, EmulationStation..., LXDE) and FliperOS Setup itself. Change it later in Setup > Games > Frontend.

FliperOS updates (without a new ISO): at every boot with internet, before the launcher, tty1 asks the repository whether there are FliperOS updates (fliperos-update check, waiting for NetworkManager and, with a cable plugged in or a saved Wi-Fi network, up to 30 s for the connection, since Wi-Fi connects after NetworkManager starts; offline or with GitHub out of reach, the boot goes on as always). Connecting to a Wi-Fi network in Setup > Network Setup runs the same check right after the connection, without waiting for the next boot. With one pending, a screen says what it brings and asks: Apply now or Not now (asked again at the next boot). Before asking it lists the files changed on this machine that the update replaces (up to six on screen, the rest in View the files): it rehearses the update in an overlay over / and compares each file it would write with what FliperOS itself installed (/var/lib/fliperos/update/manifest, the sha256 of each file, recorded by the ISO and by each update). Each of those files is copied to /var/lib/fliperos/update/backup/NNNN-date/ before being replaced (fliperos-update restore N puts them back), and the Setup choices (video, latency, buttons) are applied again afterwards. Personal files (fliperos.conf, button maps, ROMs, saves, frontend lists) aren't touched.

Updates are numbered and always applied in order: update N only goes in on a machine at N−1 (/etc/fliperos/patch-level), a list with a gap is refused, and if one fails nothing after it is applied (it is offered again at the next boot; log in /var/log/fliperos-update.log). Each one is a repository commit listed in updates/index with the sha256 of its files (downloaded over HTTPS from GitHub and checked before anything is written), plus an optional updates/NNNN.sh for what isn't a file copy. They are published with tools/make-update.sh "title" [--reboot]. An install older than this system gets it with curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/juniorterin/fliperOS/main/updates/bootstrap.sh | sudo bash.

Colour calibration (Setup > Video Setup) sets the picture's colour: everything the card can do, plus saturation and hue by software when it can't. The fifteen settings are saved in fliperos.conf as color_NAME=:

Setting Range How it is done
Brightness −50 to +50 the card's gamma table (LUT)
Contrast 50 to 150 % LUT
Gamma 0.50 to 2.00 LUT
Colour temperature 4000 to 10000 K (6500 neutral, 9300 the bluish white of many arcade monitors) LUT: red, green and blue gains from the black-body white
Red / green / blue gain (drive) 0 to 150 % LUT
Red / green / blue black level (cutoff) −50 to +50 LUT
Red / green / blue gamma 0.50 to 2.00 LUT
Saturation 0 to 200 % the card's colour matrix (CTM); on cards without one, by software
Hue −180 to +180° CTM, or by software
RGB range card default, full, limited the connector's output_csc (radeon) or Broadcast RGB (Intel), only on cards that have it

Calibrate on the screen opens test patterns in the Video Setup mode, through the same path as the emulators: - colour bars (75 % SMPTE with near-black bars); - 16 grey steps over a continuous ramp; - black level: 1, 2, 4 and 8 % bars, for brightness; - white level: 90, 95 and 98 % boxes on white, for contrast; - red, green, blue and grey ramps; - the 24 ColorChecker patches.

Up and down pick a setting and left and right change it while you look at the tube. Space hides the bar, Enter keeps the settings and Esc goes back to the previous ones. Settings in numbers changes each setting by its number (it also works over SSH), and Back to the defaults clears them all.

config/fliperos-color.c (built with the image and by each update as /usr/local/bin/fliperos-color) applies the settings. Its commands: - fliperos-color caps tells what the card has: the size of the gamma table, whether there is a CTM, which range property; - apply writes the LUT, the CTM and the range through DRM; - x11 writes the LUT to every CRTC in X; - lut N prints the table and the matrix.

Where FliperOS applies them: - at boot, as soon as the splash leaves the screen (fliperos-tty1); - before each program in fliperos-kms-run and fliperos-x11-run (an X server that closes puts back the table it found); - in X, in fliperos-x11-client and fliperos-lxde, because Xorg starts with a linear table; - when the settings are saved.

Without a CTM (the cabinet's Radeon R7 240 on the radeon driver has only the 256-point LUT and output_csc), the Setup writes color_ctm=sw. Then saturation and hue go through the same OpenGL layer as the flicker filter (libfliperos-deflicker.so, in every emulator and frontend, and in the Setup's menu), and through picom on the desktop. Only the text console stays without them.

The power button. A short press of the cabinet's power button (the motherboard's, or a panel key programmed to send KEY_POWER) depends on what is on screen:

On screen The button
A game opened from a frontend (Attract-Mode Plus, ES-DE, RetroFE, Pegasus, your own frontends) closes the game; the frontend comes back
A Fightcade match closes the match's emulator; the lobby comes back
An emulator opened from the desktop's Games menu closes the emulator; the desktop comes back
A frontend's game list, the Fightcade lobby, the desktop, the FliperOS menu, the shell, the install media powers the machine off
RetroArch or GroovyMAME as the default launcher powers the machine off (they are the launcher, there is no frontend to go back to)

From inside a game it therefore takes two presses to power off. For 3 seconds after a press the next ones don't count, so a second press given too early (or a board that reports one press through two devices) can't close the game and power off at once. The game gets a SIGTERM, the same as closing it normally, so RetroArch still writes its saves; a game that stays open is killed (SIGKILL) by the next press. Holding the button for 4 seconds still cuts the power: that is the motherboard, not FliperOS.

How it works: systemd-logind ignores the button (HandlePowerKey=ignore in /etc/systemd/logind.conf.d/fliperos.conf; by default it would power off at every press), and triggerhappy, which already reads the volume keys, runs config/fliperos-powerkey on KEY_POWER (config/fliperos-power.triggers). The script finds the open game through the FLIPEROS_POWERKEY variable that fliperos-session gives what it opens: frontend (the game is whatever runs below it), game (an emulator opened from the desktop) or games:FILE (Fightcade, whose emulators are not its children, lists their PIDs in FILE).

Volume keys: the keyboard's volume keys (or those of a panel encoder programmed to send them) work on any screen — emulator in KMS, desktop, console. triggerhappy reads /dev/input and calls fliperos-setup --volume up|down|mute (5% steps on the Audio Setup card, saved on every press because cabinets are usually switched off at the wall); config/fliperos-volume.triggers says which keys. The LXDE panel only handles the mouse, so the volume doesn't change twice. No volume bar appears during a game (nothing draws on top of an emulator in KMS).

Web panel

The cabinet's state in a browser on the same network (a phone too): http://fliperos.local:8081, or the cabinet's IP with :8081. You sign in with the same user and password as SSH and the network share (fliperos, changed in Setup > System > Password). Until 2026-10-22 the panel was on port 8080, which Pixelcade's own settings page also uses; with both installed one of them didn't open. The panel has seven pages, tabs at the top, each with its own address (#frontend, #files, #live, #romfilter, #settings, #status, #terminal); the browser remembers the last one: - Frontend: now playing with the FPS, games, volume and screen; - Files: files, downloads and tasks; - Live: the cabinet screen live, the streaming controls (service, encoder, sound) and the addresses for OBS (the live screen and the marquee). Leaving the page stops the stream, so ffmpeg on the cabinet stops too; - ROM filter: the MAME ROM Cleaner filter, for the Downloader or for a romset folder; - Settings: colour values are sliders; - Status: now playing and the screen at the top, then video, CPU, GPU, memory, storage, network, processes and logs; - Terminal.

The status refreshes every second. What each part shows: - Now playing: the game open (emulator, file, RetroArch core) and the frontend, with the FPS and a graph of the last minute. The FPS comes from the OpenGL layer of the flicker filter, which counts the frames of whatever game is open. That runs on the graphics card (VA-API) when it has an encoder; otherwise in software with x264, ultrafast, no B-frames (cheap at 640x240). The R7 240 has no encoder: its Oland chip has no VCE. On the cabinet (an i7 870) x264 keeps 60 frames per second, and the browser on the network gets them with about 13 ms in its buffer. The pixels stay sharp, as on the tube. The frame is copied in RGB and enlarged with nearest-neighbour to 1280x960 (4:3) before it is encoded: 640x240 becomes blocks of 2x4, 480i 2x2, and RetroArch's 2560x240 halves across. On the cabinet's HDA card it doesn't play while another program holds the card through the system's dmix. - The copy only goes into asound.conf with the loopback really there. Debug and latency rewrite the kernel line the way their screens do; their kernel part counts after a restart. - Monitor, resolution and video driver are not here, since a wrong choice can leave the cabinet without a picture. The emulator of a romset folder is guessed from its name, as in the Setup. - The parameters are a board of tiles in groups (which games, players and controls, screen, categories and years, hardware), like the Setup's graphical filter board. It is a service in Setup > System > Services (Web panel). By default it is After boot, so it starts about 40 s after the boot, with the game already on screen. config/fliperos-web (installed as /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-web, the fliperos-web.service unit) uses only Python's standard library, on port 8081 (--port changes it; 8080 is Pixelcade's own settings page). It runs as root to read DRM and to check the password in /etc/shadow through libcrypt, the same way the login does. Only the fliperos user signs in. The session is an HttpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie that lasts 12 hours. SSH and AI assistants: SSH is on from the first boot, and SFTP too. From another computer on the same network:

Field Value
Address (host) fliperos.local, or the cabinet's IP (shown in the FliperOS menu title)
User (login, username) fliperos
Password fliperos until you change it in Setup > System > Password
Port 22

The user and the password are the same word by default, but they are two separate things. In the command ssh fliperos@fliperos.local, the fliperos before the @ is the user and fliperos.local after it is the address; the password is not in the command, you type it when SSH asks (fliperos@fliperos.local's password:), and nothing appears on screen while you type. In an SFTP program (FileZilla, WinSCP) the same four fields are Host, Username, Password and Port. sudo asks for the same password, and the network share (\\fliperos\roms) takes the same user and password. After a password change only the password is different: the user is still fliperos.

Under the menus it's a regular Ubuntu 24.04, so whatever the Setup doesn't cover can be done from a terminal, and an AI assistant with shell access (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex and the like) can do it for you: give it the cabinet's address and say what you want (button mapping, an emulator's settings, a frontend theme, a service) and it configures the system over SSH. The configuration is in plain text files (/etc/fliperos/fliperos.conf, ~/config, /opt/fliperos), easy for a person or an assistant to read and change. On a network you don't control, change the default password in Setup > System > Password (it changes the network share's too), and turn off what you don't use in Setup > System > Services.

Pixelcade marquee

A Pixelcade LED or LCD marquee shows the game you have selected in the frontend and the game you open. Setup > System > Extras > Pixelcade installs it. Pixelcade's software (pixelweb) is closed source, so it isn't in the image: the Setup downloads it from Pixelcade's GitHub (alinke/pixelcade-linux-builds), along with the artwork (a few hundred MB), into ~/pixelcade. Choosing Extras > Pixelcade again, once it is installed, offers to update the program and the artwork, or to install the paid art pack: type the license code you got from Pixelcade and the Setup downloads the pack. ~/pixelcade is the folder Pixelcade's own guides use, so the same can be done by hand: cd ~/pixelcade && ./pixelweb -install-artpack <code>. The events below are the ones of Pixelcade's own EmulationStation scripts (alinke/pixelcade-linux), with the same calls to pixelweb.

  • Service: fliperos-pixelcade.service keeps pixelweb running as the fliperos user and starts it when the marquee is plugged in (99-fliperos-pixelcade.rules, USB IDs 1b4f:0008 for V1 and 2e8a:1050 for V2). The serial port belongs to the dialout group. Pixelcade's settings page is at http://<cabinet IP>:8080. If no marquee is connected, pixelweb searches for about 30 seconds and then exits. The service tries 3 times and then stops until the marquee is plugged in. When it starts, the marquee shows the FliperOS logo; when the computer shuts down or restarts, it shows "Bye".
  • Game opened: every game from every frontend and emulator goes through fliperos-kms-run or fliperos-x11-run. These call fliperos-pixelcade launch in the background, so a game never waits for the marquee. The game is the last argument: a file in ~/roms, or the set name for GroovyMAME and Model 2. When there is no game (a frontend, an emulator's menu) nothing happens. First a text scrolls once: the game's high scores, or "Now Playing" and the game's name. Then the game's marquee stays.
  • High scores: for arcade games, the text is the top three scores (#1 19,130 DAA #2 ...). They come from the game's .hi file, the newest one among ~/.mame/hiscore (GroovyMAME's hiscore plugin) and the RetroArch saves folder (/opt/fliperos/saves/retroarch, where the MAME and FBNeo cores write theirs). The hi2txt program reads the file, as in Pixelcade's installer. Extras > Pixelcade downloads it (pinned by hash) into ~/pixelcade/hi2txt and installs Java from apt (default-jre-headless), which it needs. A game that hi2txt doesn't know shows its name instead.
  • Game closed: the helper waits for the game to end and then shows the game's marquee the way the frontend selection does. That matters in Pegasus and RetroFE, which don't report what is selected.
  • Game selected: in ES-DE, through its custom event scripts (~/ES-DE/scripts/game-select and system-select, with Custom event scripts and Browsing custom events turned on; browsing doesn't wait for them). In Attract-Mode Plus, through FliperOS's Pixelcade plugin, which is copied to ~/.attract/plugins and turned on in plugins.cfg. Pegasus and RetroFE have no event hooks, so they only show the game that was opened. The service writes these frontend settings at every boot; if the frontend is open at that moment, they wait for the next boot.
  • Screensaver: when the screensaver of ES-DE (screensaver-start and screensaver-end scripts) or Attract-Mode Plus starts, the marquee goes into Pixelcade's attract mode, a playlist you set up on the settings page. When it ends, the marquee shows the selected game or system again.
  • Achievements: with RetroAchievements turned on in RetroArch (cheevos_enable = "true"), each unlocked achievement shows on the marquee, and then the game's marquee comes back. RetroArch has no event for this, so fliperos-kms-run opens it with --verbose and the helper reads the [RCHEEVOS] Awarding achievement lines from /opt/fliperos/logs/retroarch.log. In debug mode the log goes to the screen and no achievement is shown.
  • Pixelcade LCD over USB: the LCD marquee (USB ID 1d6b:3232, firmware 6.3 or newer) appears as a network card. The udev rule keeps it out of NetworkManager and starts fliperos-pixelcade-lcd@<interface>.service, which gives the card the address 169.254.100.2 (the LCD is 169.254.100.1), sets lcdMarquee and lcdUsbConnected in ~/pixelcade/pixelcade.ini, writes the LCD's name there and restarts the service. This is what Pixelcade's installer does; it has not been tested with an LCD marquee yet.
  • Visual Pinball: a program whose name starts with vpinball draws the display itself, over the same serial port. When it opens, pixelweb (and dmdserver) close and let go of the port; when it ends, the services open them again. FliperOS doesn't ship Visual Pinball.
  • Systems: the arcade folders (mame, naomi, naomi2, atomiswave, model2, model3, and the MAME and FBNeo cores) use Pixelcade's mame artwork. The RetroArch cores go by their system (snes, genesis, psx...). Other folders go by their name.

DOFLinx (effects during the game)

DOFLinx shows the score and effects on the Pixelcade marquee while a game runs. It is closed source and large (about 230 MB to download), so it is a separate extra: Setup > System > Extras > DOFLinx, after Pixelcade. It follows Pixelcade's own setup, which covers the FBNeo arcade games in RetroArch.

  • What is installed: the Linux program from DOFLinx's GitHub (DOFLinx/CurrentExecutable) into ~/doflinx, the DOFLinx.ini and colours.ini that Pixelcade publishes, with FliperOS's folders, in ~/doflinx/config, and the per-game files, which pixelweb -update-doflinx downloads into ~/pixelcade/DOFLinx. If you change DOFLinx.ini, a later install keeps your file and saves the new one as DOFLinx.ini.latest.
  • RetroArch: DOFLinx finds the running game and reads its memory through RetroArch's Network Command Interface. The install writes /etc/fliperos/retroarch/doflinx.cfg (network_cmd_enable, port 55355), which fliperos-kms-run adds to RetroArch. Without DOFLinx the interface stays off.
  • Service: fliperos-doflinx.service runs it as the fliperos user after pixelweb answers.
  • It has not been tested with a marquee yet.

ZeDMD (open-source LED display)

ZeDMD is an open-source LED display you can build yourself: an ESP32 board driving HUB75 LED panels, 128x32 (two 64x32 panels) or 256x64. Setup > System > Extras > ZeDMD installs support for it. The display gets the same events as the Pixelcade marquee: the game selected in ES-DE or Attract-Mode Plus, the system selected in ES-DE, the game opened from any frontend or emulator (with the high scores or "Now Playing" scrolling first), the game closed, the screensaver, the RetroAchievements and "Bye" at shutdown. Pixelcade and ZeDMD can be installed together. The high scores need hi2txt and Java, which come with Extras > Pixelcade.

  • What is installed: dmdserver from libdmdutil (the Visual Pinball DMD library, pinned version and hash) into ~/zedmd, and the python3-pil package. Neither is in the image.
  • Service: fliperos-zedmd.service runs dmdserver as the fliperos user, listening on 127.0.0.1:6789. The udev rule (99-fliperos-zedmd.rules) names the ESP32's USB serial adapter /dev/zedmd and starts the service when it is plugged in (CP210x 10c4:ea60, CH340 1a86:7523, CH9102 1a86:55d4, ESP32-S3 native USB 303a:1001). With another adapter, dmdserver searches every USB serial port. If no ZeDMD is found, the service tries 3 times and then stops until one is plugged in.
  • What it shows: fliperos-dmd sends a 128x32 picture (dmdserver scales it up for a 256x64 panel). It looks in ~/media in this order: dmd/<games folder>/<rom>.png or .gif (dmd/mame/sf2.gif; the same folders as ~/roms) (art made for the display; GIFs play animated), then the scraped marquee and logo images (Setup > Games > Scraper). Without any picture it shows the game's name, taken from the Attract-Mode or ES-DE game list, scrolling when it doesn't fit. A system picture goes in ~/media/dmd/<system>.png.
  • Screensaver and startup: during the frontend's screensaver the display cycles through the pictures in ~/media/dmd and ~/media/marquee, in random order, 8 seconds each. When the service starts it shows the FliperOS logo.
  • Artwork: Pixelcade's artwork is licensed only for Pixelcade hardware, so FliperOS doesn't use it on the ZeDMD.

Versus score on the LED display

During a two-player match the display shows the score: the matches each side has won since the game opened. On a ZeDMD the score stays on the display (GUILE 3 x 1 KEN, with each side's character when the game tells it, or P1 and P2). On a Pixelcade a text scrolls each time someone wins a match, and then the game's marquee comes back. The score appears with the first win: until then (the game's demo, the character select, the first match) the display keeps the game's picture.

It works in GroovyMAME, for about 200 arcade ROM sets, and in RetroArch with the FBNeo core, for about 100: the ones Fightcade has a detector for. The full list is in Scoreboard games.

What was checked on a FliperOS cabinet, with Fightcade's own detectors and no picture on the tube:

  • GroovyMAME: Street Fighter II' Champion Edition, The King of Fighters '98, Mortal Kombat II and Street Fighter III 3rd Strike, one of each kind of detector, played by a script. The wins of both players and the characters came out right, and so did the 128x32 picture sent to the display.
  • RetroArch (FBNeo): Street Fighter II' CE, KOF '98 and 3rd Strike left in their demo. The memory the detectors describe was read right (the match start and the characters). No match was played there, because nothing can press the buttons of a RetroArch without picture, so a win has not been seen counted in RetroArch.

  • A real LED display (ZeDMD or Pixelcade) showing the score has not been seen yet.

  • Where the score comes from: the game only draws it on the screen, so it is read from the game's memory. The map is Fightcade's detectors, one file per game saying where a match starts, where each side wins and which character each side chose. It is the map Fightcade uses to know who won each online match. Its repository has no license, so the files aren't in the image: Extras > Pixelcade and Extras > ZeDMD download them (a pinned version) into ~/.local/share/fliperos/versus-detectors. fliperos-versus fetch downloads them again.

  • Which games: the CPS1 and CPS2 games (Street Fighter II, Alpha 3, Marvel vs. Capcom...), the Neo Geo ones (The King of Fighters, Garou, Samurai Shodown...) the CPS3 ones (Street Fighter III, JoJo, Red Earth) and the ones whose detector uses the CPU's own addresses (Mortal Kombat II and many others; these only in GroovyMAME). A clone uses its parent's detector.
  • How it is read: for a game with a detector, the groovymame command turns on the fliperos-versus plugin. The plugin evaluates the detector every frame, the way Fightcade's emulator does (a condition counts after 30 frames in a row), and writes the result (fliperos_wins1, fliperos_wins2, fliperos_char1, fliperos_char2) to a file in the session's runtime folder, which fliperos-versus follows. These are not MAME outputs and don't go through port 8000: in today's MAME a script can't create new outputs, only use the ones the game declares.
  • Points: the detectors don't have each player's points. For those there is /etc/fliperos/versus-games.conf, a table of addresses read directly (the format is explained at its top; your own lines go in ~/config/versus-games.conf). It is empty for now, so the display shows the wins only. When a game has its points there, they appear below the wins.
  • RetroArch: with the FBNeo core, the emulator the detectors were written for. fliperos-kms-run turns on RetroArch's Network Command Interface for a game with a detector (/etc/fliperos/retroarch/versus.cfg), and fliperos-versus reads the game's memory ten times a second (READ_CORE_RAM) and evaluates the detector itself. It covers the CPS1, CPS2, Neo Geo and CPS3 games; a detector that uses the CPU's own addresses can't be read there. The MAME cores (mame2010, mame) don't let the memory be read, so there is no score in them: use GroovyMAME or FBNeo for those games.
  • The FBNeo core comes in the image from 0.9.1. On an installed system, get it in RetroArch: Online Updater > Core Downloader > Arcade (FinalBurn Neo). Its games go in ~/roms/retroarch/fbneo, in FBNeo's own ROM sets. The Fightcade ROMs (~/roms/fightcade) are Fightcade's alone: the frontends don't list them, and the core's folder is not linked to them (an earlier FliperOS made that link by itself; it is removed at the next boot, and the folder is empty again). To play a set in the core, copy it to ~/roms/retroarch/fbneo; a set that changed between Fightcade's FBNeo 0.2.97.44 and the current core may not open in the core.
  • Console sets in the FBNeo folder. The core only looks for a .zip among the arcade sets, unless the folder it is in has the name of a system (megadrive, sms, nes, pce, tg16, sgx, msx, coleco, sg1000, gamegear, spectrum, fds, snes, ngp, channelf); a Mega Drive game loose among the arcade sets closed with romset is unknown. Fightcade ROM packs often come like that, with every system in one folder. FliperOS finds the system of each set in FBNeo's own lists (/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-fbneo; a name that exists in more than one system is settled by the CRCs inside the .zip) and opens the game through a link in ~/.cache/fliperos-fbneo/<system>/, with its parent set and the system BIOS (spectrum.zip, msx.zip, coleco.zip...) linked next to it, so nothing has to be moved. This happens when a frontend or the Games menu opens the game; a set loaded by hand in RetroArch's own menu still has to be in a system folder. A set that is in none of FBNeo's current lists still shows romset is unknown.
  • Model 2 (SM2-Emu): for Virtua Fighter 2, Fighting Vipers, Sonic the Fighters, Last Bronx and Dead or Alive, the emulator itself reads the memory, with FliperOS's patch, and publishes the rounds each side has taken among the game's outputs (P1_Wins, P2_Wins on TCP port 8000, see DIY hardware). With a display installed, fliperos-x11-run turns that port on for these games and fliperos-versus follows it. A match is counted for the side with more rounds when they go back to zero, which the game does when the next match starts, so the win appears then; there are no characters. Checked with the real emulator up to the rounds arriving and with a scripted feed for the count; not seen in a match on a cabinet. fliperos-versus watch model2 <game> shows the values.
  • Sides: versus_side=2 in /etc/fliperos/fliperos.conf puts player 2 first, for the cabinet on that side.
  • Checking a game: open it and run fliperos-versus watch mame <game> over SSH: it prints the wins and the characters as they change, to compare with the screen.
  • The count starts again when the game closes. Only with a Pixelcade or a ZeDMD installed; without one, nothing is turned on.

APT repository

The frontends (Attract-Mode Plus, EmulationStation, RetroFE, Pegasus) come from a dedicated APT repository of prebuilt .debs, like GroovyArcade's groovy-ux-repo (packaging/build-deb.sh, packaging/make-repo.sh, Dockerfile.packages, recipes in packaging/packages/). The config/fliperos-sessions.conf table says, for each launcher, whether it runs in KMS or X (from galauncher's videodata.conf table) and which package it comes from. The build carries the repository into the image (/opt/fliperos/repo, deb [trusted=yes] file:/opt/fliperos/repo ./, option --repo) and already installs Attract-Mode Plus; without it, the cabinet's apt said "Unable to locate package". Attract-Mode Plus, EmulationStation (ES-DE) and Pegasus have recipes; RetroFE doesn't yet, and shows in Setup > Games > Frontend as not available yet.

GroovyMAME: the release and the groovymame command. The build no longer compiles GroovyMAME (it took over an hour): it uses the official Linux release (gm0289sr222f, MAME 0.289 with Switchres 2.22f, verified by sha256), in /usr/local/libexec/groovymame, with the libraries it needs (Qt6 is the debugger's). The groovymame command — the one fliperos-launch, the frontends and the terminal call — is config/fliperos-groovymame, which passes -inipath /etc/fliperos/mame: the release's default is .;ini, relative to the folder it is opened from, so a ~/.mame/mame.ini doesn't apply; what applies is /etc/fliperos/mame/mame.ini (owned by the fliperos user), which the Setup writes. It also preloads libfliperos-sdl-audio.so (config/fliperos-sdl-audio.c). MAME 0.289 routes each game's speaker to the output SDL calls the default and saves that choice in the game's cfg (sound_map). SDL 2.30's ALSA backend cannot name a default and lists only the hw: devices, so MAME took the card's raw hw: device: no dmix shared with other programs, not the card picked in the Setup, and no sound on the live screen. The library reports ALSA's default as SDL's default. On the first game with it, the command also removes the old sound_map entries from ~/.mame/cfg once (marker .fliperos-sound-default). MAME's portaudio module is no fix: it never lists default either, and opens the card's hw: device, silent while another program holds the card. The release only ships the binary; like GroovyArcade's package, the build puts in /usr/local/share/groovymame the source files of the same tag (commit 953db38): plugins (hiscore), the uismall.bdf font, bgfx, artwork, language, ctrlr, keymaps and hash. mame.ini is made like GroovyArcade's: GroovyMAME's own -createconfig (every option of the version, at its default), with those of config/mame.ini on top, each on its own line (config/fliperos-mame-ini, at build time). The GroovyArcade options (gasetup's -createconfig): plugin hiscore, skip_gameinfo 1, video opengl, switchres_ini 1, sound sdl, aspect 4:3 — plus the Setup's latency (lowlatency, autoframedelay, framedelay); the UI font stays MAME's own (uifont default), because GroovyArcade's pixel font (uismall.bdf) was too small on the cabinet. modesetting 1 is also GroovyArcade's, with GroovyMAME in X (the "GroovyMAME at native resolution" paragraph). What GroovyMAME writes (cfg, nvram, hiscore records, savestates) goes to ~/.mame; ui.ini gets GroovyArcade's infos_text_size 1.00 and font_rows 20, together with the Dracula colors — GroovyArcade uses 19, but MAME only accepts 20 to 40 and replaces 19 with the default 30, which at 224 lines leaves the font 7 pixels tall and unreadable; with 20 it is 11. On an earlier installation, fliperos-rootfs.sh only adds to mame.ini and ui.ini the keys they don't have yet (what was already saved stays, theme included); among the paths, the relative ones (the ., cfg, snap of a plain -createconfig: relative to the folder GroovyMAME is opened from, they filled the home folder with cfg and nvram) are removed, and with no absolute one left, the one from config/ applies. The cfg/nvram left in the home folder moves to ~/.mame; when both exist, each file keeps its newest version. On a system installed earlier, fliperos-rootfs.sh moves the compiled binary to /usr/local/libexec and puts the shortcut in its place.

Cards without low dotclocks (Intel, NVIDIA). They cannot generate the ~12 MHz pixel clock of a 640x240 mode at 15 kHz, so the Setup writes dotclock_min 25.0 to /etc/switchres.ini. Running a program at 640x240

On a 15 kHz monitor, 480 lines only exist interlaced (480i), and interlacing flickers. At 640x240 progressive the program draws its 480 lines in 240: it loses half the vertical resolution, but the image is stable — that's how FliperOS runs Dreamcast, PS2, GameCube/Wii and laserdisc at 15 kHz. The one doing this is fliperos-x11-run: it opens the program in an Xorg of its own, and Switchres creates the requested mode within the ranges of the monitor chosen in Video Setup.

Three ways, from the one-off to the permanent:

  1. On the spot, in the console (Setup > Exit to shell, on the cabinet):

sh /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run --mode 640x240@60 program [arguments]

Any mode works (WIDTHxHEIGHT@HZ, e.g. 320x240@60, 640x240@59.94, 384x224@59.64).

  1. In the desktop menu: a shortcut in ~/.local/share/applications/my-program.desktop. fliperos-launch --mode leaves the desktop, opens the program in the requested mode and returns to the desktop when it closes (with the same confirmation as the menu's emulators):

ini [Desktop Entry] Type=Application Name=My program Exec=/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-launch --mode 640x240@60 /path/to/the/program Categories=Game;

  1. Always for that program: a line in /etc/fliperos/emulator-modes.conf (command name, mode at 15 kHz, mode on the other monitors). From then on any path that goes through fliperos-x11-run — the menu, a frontend, the console without --mode — uses that mode:

myemulator 640x240@60 640x480@60

A new image doesn't overwrite this file once it has been edited.

In a frontend (Attract-Mode and others), the emulator command becomes /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run --mode 640x240@60 emulator "[romfilename]" (or without --mode, with the line in the table).

The image has to be stretched. 640x240 is an 8:3 screen in pixels, which the tube shows as 4:3. A program that "keeps the 4:3 aspect ratio" would use only half the width (320 columns) and come out squashed in the middle. Set the program to stretch/fill the screen — for Flycast (rend.ScreenStretching = 200, via -config), Dolphin (AspectRatio = 3, via -C), PCSX2 (AspectRatio = Stretch, in PCSX2.ini from the second launch on) and Model 2 (aspect_mode = stretch) fliperos-x11-run already does it by itself. The math, for another mode: stretch by 300 × width ÷ (4 × height) percent.

GroovyMAME and RetroArch don't go through here: they switch modes by themselves on every game. In GroovyMAME, interlace 0 in /etc/fliperos/mame/mame.ini makes Switchres never choose an interlaced mode (480-line games come out at 240p). In RetroArch, the MAME 2010 core already ships that way: CRT SwitchRes reads a switchres.ini of the core only, on top of /etc/switchres.ini, in ~/.config/retroarch/config/MAME 2010/MAME 2010.switchres.ini (from config/retroarch-switchres), with interlace 0 — a 640x480 game (Namco System 12) opens at 640x240 progressive, with the image shrunk vertically, instead of 480i. The same file named after another core (config/<Core>/<Core>.switchres.ini) does the same for it; to go back to 480i, interlace 1 and without the # FliperOS line at the top (updates only rewrite the file that has it).

480i games: 640x240, filter or pure 480i

480i flicker filter

When a game does run interlaced (480i on a 15 kHz tube), a line one pixel tall exists in only one of the two fields, so it is drawn 30 times a second and flickers. The filter blends each line with its neighbour before the frame goes out. Then every detail is in both fields: the flicker goes away and the image is a little softer. - Medium, the default, averages each line with the next one. That is RePlayOS's "Interlaced Flicker Reduction", which samples between two lines. - Light (15/70/15) keeps more detail. - Strong (a third each from the line above, the line and the line below) hides the most. - Off leaves the picture sharp, with the flicker.

The level is per program, in the 480i games table above.

The filter finds the level from the first of these that is set: 1. a file named by FLIPEROS_DEFLICKER_FILE, re-read every 30 frames (the preview); 2. FLIPEROS_DEFLICKER; 3. the open RetroArch core: deflicker_pcsx_rearmed=. The filter lists the loaded libraries every 30 frames to see which *_libretro.so is open. It does not wrap dlopen, which would change where each program looks for its own libraries; 4. the program: deflicker_flycast=. The name comes from FLIPEROS_DEFLICKER_APP, which fliperos-x11-run sets to the table name, or else from the process; 5. deflicker=.

It works below the emulators, so no emulator needs a shader of its own. libfliperos-deflicker.so (config/fliperos-deflicker.c, built with the image and on each update into /usr/local/lib/fliperos) is loaded through LD_PRELOAD by fliperos-kms-run (RetroArch and the other KMS emulators) and by fliperos-x11-client (the emulators in their own Xorg). It is not loaded into Xorg, into Switchres or into the 32-bit Wine programs. The library catches the frame swap (glXSwapBuffers, eglSwapBuffers, including lookups through dlsym and GetProcAddress, as SDL does). It copies the frame, draws it back through a three-tap vertical filter and restores the GL state the emulator had. It checks the CRTC mode through DRM every 30 frames: in a progressive mode (240p, 31 kHz, LCD) it does nothing. The filter works with OpenGL (compatibility, core and GLES), not with Vulkan, so when it is on for them and the mode is interlaced, fliperos-x11-run puts PCSX2 (Renderer = 12), Dolphin (GFXBackend=OGL) and Flycast (Vulkan pvr.rend 4 and 5 become OpenGL's 0 and 3, for that launch only) on OpenGL. The LXDE desktop draws without OpenGL, so it gets the same filter through picom with a window shader (config/fliperos-desktop-deflicker, started by fliperos-lxde only when the screen mode is interlaced, at the Desktop row's level deflicker_desktop=).

For testing: - FLIPEROS_DEFLICKER=off|light|medium|strong overrides the level. - FLIPEROS_DEFLICKER_FORCE=1 filters in any mode. - FLIPEROS_DEFLICKER_DEBUG=1 prints the level it picked (with the program and the core) and the size and GL profile it filters. - tools/deflicker-test.sh builds the library and checks the result on a software-rendered Xvfb. It covers the per-program and per-core levels, the desktop shader and the preview with its arrows.

OpenBOR

The games (.pak) go in /opt/fliperos/roms/openbor/Paks (over the network: \\fliperos\FliperOS\roms\openbor\Paks). The Games > OpenBOR menu opens OpenBOR's own menu, which lists the paks.

Old paks. OpenBOR is version 4. Many mods were made for 3.0 (build 6391) and stop as soon as they load in 4 (the log in Logs/OpenBorLog.txt says An Error Occurred, e.g. Unable to load image file '...gif': 4 no longer takes GIF sprites). To try a pak in 4 again, remove its line from paks.txt. The 3.0 binary is in the repository (config/openbor-legacy, rebuilt by tools/build-openbor-legacy.sh), so updates bring it too.

Model 2

SM2-Emu is the Sega Model 2 emulator (native, BSD). It is not compiled into the image. Games > Model 2 is always in the menu. The first click opens a terminal and asks to clone and build it (the network, once; a few minutes). What is missing from the compiler (glslc, ninja, the X and ALSA headers the vendored SDL3 needs) is installed then, with sudo. The tree stays in ~/.local/src/sm2-emu, owned by the user. After that, the same screen lists the .zip files in ~/roms/model2 by the names in games.xml, and Update Model 2 at the bottom pulls and rebuilds. Esc cancels and the desktop stays. A set that cannot be loaded stays on that screen, with the missing chip named in the header, instead of closing back to the desktop.

It opens in its own Xorg, full screen, in the same mode as Supermodel. At 15 kHz that is 640x240, and each launch sets aspect_mode = stretch so the frame fills the tube. VSync is on by default (vsync = true, set on the first launch); turning it off in F10 sticks. Chips are matched by CRC32, so the zip has to be a MAME Model 2 set: one made only for the old Model 2 Emulator can be missing a chip, and that chip is named on the game list (and in /opt/fliperos/logs/sm2-emu.log). A gamepad is read by button position through SDL, with the keyboard live beside it (5 and 6 insert coins, 1 and 2 start, arrows and Z X C V are player 1). F9 quits back to the desktop; F10 is the emulator's own settings.

It is CPU-bound on one core. FliperOS builds it with link-time optimization. The speed work below started as FliperOS patches and is part of SM2-Emu itself since 0.9.41, so they left config/sm2-emu-patches (installed to /usr/local/share/fliperos/sm2-emu-patches); a patch put there is applied before compiling. The one there now publishes game state as outputs: the recoil, ammunition and life of the gun games for light guns, the round wins of the fighting games and the credits (DIY hardware). Most of the time on the 2B and 2C boards went to the geometry coprocessor waiting for its next command: the Model 2C MB86235 re-ran a read of its empty input FIFO every cycle, and the Model 2B SHARC spun in a one-NOP DO ... UNTIL NOT FLAG0_IN loop. Nothing else runs during that slice, so each repeat leaves the machine in the same state. The emulator now skips to the end of the slice instead. The i960 also fetches instructions straight from ROM and work RAM, without a virtual bus call. The emulated machine is unchanged: a 600-frame run gives the same final state and the same audio, bit for bit, on House of the Dead, Last Bronx, Sonic Championship and Virtua Fighter 2. On the cabinet's i3-540 (headless, one core), the speed went from 0.93× to 2.34× on House of the Dead (2C), 0.71× to 1.65× on Last Bronx (2B), 0.80× to 2.35× on Sonic Championship (2B), 0.85× to 2.84× on Fighting Vipers (2B) and 2.42× to 3.15× on Virtua Fighter 2 (2A). The F10 settings menu stays readable on a 640x240 screen (also upstream now): the overlay no longer shrinks its font below the native 13 pixels, and the Settings window fits between the menu bar and the status bar, scrolling what does not fit. A patch that no longer applies to a newer SM2-Emu is skipped, and the build goes on without it. When a FliperOS update brings a different set of patches, the next Games > Model 2 offers to update: it pulls SM2-Emu and rebuilds (the network, a few minutes), because a patch that left FliperOS now comes from upstream. Since 0.9.43 the settings menu is translated, and the build also asks for the HarfBuzz and fontconfig headers. The quick check of a set before it opens uses a throwaway NVRAM, so it does not overwrite the game's saved one. OpenGL 4.3 needs a GPU that can open that context (that cabinet's R7 240 opened a 4.5 core context).

Fightcade 2

Fightcade 2 (online arcade and console matches, with rollback) is closed-source freeware with no clear permission to redistribute — it isn't in the image or in the repository. In Setup > Games > Frontend it shows as Fightcade 2 (not installed): when chosen, the Setup downloads the official Linux package from its website (about 160 MB, https://www.fightcade.com/download/linux), with a progress bar, to /opt/fliperos/fightcade — a user-owned folder, because Fightcade updates itself by writing there. In the sessions table the package column is fetch:fliperos-fightcade (a program downloaded from its website, not a .deb), and the line's "binary", /opt/fliperos/fightcade/fightcade, only exists after the download. Once installed it can be the boot launcher and shows in Games > Fightcade 2 in LXDE. /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-fightcade fetch downloads it again on top (repair): the ROMs and settings stay.

  • ROMs in ~/roms/fightcade, one folder per emulator it uses: fbneo (the .zip files of FBNeo 0.2.97.44, with neogeo.zip; console games in subfolders megadrive, nes, sms...), flycast (Naomi, Atomiswave, Dreamcast), snes9x and fc1 (the Fightcade 1 games, FB Alpha 0.2.96.74 romset). The ROMs folders inside Fightcade become links to them (config/fliperos-roms); the folders exist even before installing. RetroArch's FBNeo core shares the fbneo folder while its own (~/roms/retroarch/fbneo) has no games. The romsets are the ones each emulator asks for, not those of the current MAME.
  • Lobby screen: the lobby is a desktop page (a 1280x800 window) and doesn't fit in 240 lines, so it is the only program in the modes table that opens interlaced at 15 kHz: 512x448 (fightcade 512x448@60 in /etc/fliperos/emulator-modes.conf; wider pixels than 640x480 for bigger text, and an interlaced 15 kHz mode can't have much fewer lines), with the page scaled down in proportion to the height (--force-device-scale-factor, 100% at 600 lines, 0.75 at 448; FIGHTCADE_SCALE sets another value, and Text > Zoom In/Out, Ctrl+= and Ctrl+-, adjusts it on the fly).
  • Matches in the game's native resolution: on a 15 kHz tube, every FBNeo and FBA (Fightcade 1) game plays in its own original resolution, like GroovyMAME does in GroovyArcade — Street Fighter II (CPS) at 384x224 at 59.637 Hz, Neo Geo at 320x224, and so on — with a modeline made for it by Switchres. Nothing is scaled: each pixel of the board is one pixel on the tube, with no stretching to 320 or 640 and no 16:9 window. How it works: when FBNeo or FBA opens, fliperos-fightcade pauses it (before Wine reads its settings), takes the game's size and refresh from GroovyMAME's -listxml (the romset names are the same; the answer is cached in ~/.cache/fliperos/fightcade-modes), adds the mode to the output with Switchres and turns on the emulator's ArcadeRes option (bVidArcaderesHor in fcadefbneo.ini, bVidArcaderes in FBA's); then it lets the emulator go on, and Wine switches the screen to the game's mode. It works the same in an online match and in Test game. Vertical games, games GroovyMAME doesn't know and widths the modeline can't make exact use the table's mode instead (fightcade-fbneo and fightcade-fc1, 320x240), at the game's own refresh rate when GroovyMAME knows it. The screen and the emulator run at the same rate: the emulators run each game at its original speed (bForce60Hz 0, which FliperOS sets once in FBA, where it comes turned on), and the output keeps only one mode of each size, because the emulators ask Wine for a size, not for a refresh rate (CPS2 and CPS3 are both 384x224, at 59.637 and 59.599 Hz). FIGHTCADE_NATIVE=0 turns it off.
  • The other emulators also open in their native modes from the table: SNES9x at 256x224 at 60.0988 Hz (the console's own mode, fightcade-snes9x) and Flycast at 640x240, stretched to 200% like the system Flycast (fightcade-flycast). fliperos-fightcade checks every second whether one of its emulators is open: if so, Switchres computes the modeline and xrandr switches the screen to its mode; when it closes, it goes back to the lobby's. For each emulator to fill the mode, its file (created from the package's default) gets full screen at that size on each launch, following the table, and, only the first time (whatever is changed later in the emulator itself stays), the game already in full screen (bVidAutoSwitchFull 1, fullscreen = yes, SNES9x's EmulateFullscreen) and, on a tube, a 4:3 monitor instead of the package's 16:9. On the other monitors (25/31 kHz, LCD) the matches stay in the lobby's mode.
  • Pure picture: scanlines, bilinear filtering, shaders, motion blur, hardware gamma and SNES9x's hi-res blending are turned off once (each emulator keeps what you change later), with VSync on.
  • Keys: it needs a keyboard and mouse (the login, the lobby). Ctrl+W (Window > Close) closes Fightcade and returns to the menu; Alt+Tab switches between the lobby and the match's emulator and Alt+F4 closes the front window (config/openbox-fightcade.xml).
  • Wine: the FBNeo, SNES9x and FBA emulators are 32-bit Windows programs; they run in Wine, which isn't in the image: downloading Fightcade through the Setup installs it first (or Setup > System > Extras), with a prefix of their own (~/.local/share/fliperos/wine-fightcade), without the Mono/Gecko window. 32-bit Wine's Direct3D draws through 32-bit OpenGL (libgl1:i386, libgl1-mesa-dri:i386, libglx-mesa0:i386, installed together with Wine): without it FBNeo stops at "Couldn't initialise DirectX9 Alternate video output". The prefix is created the first time the lobby opens (about 15 s), not on the first match. Fightcade's Flycast (Flycast Dojo) is native, but ships as an AppImage, which asks for FUSE: the launcher exports APPIMAGE_EXTRACT_AND_RUN=1, and it extracts itself to /tmp on each launch and runs from there. Every time the lobby opens, Dojo receives the system Flycast's video and control options (~/.config/flycast/emu.cfg): every rend.* and pvr.* option — no linear interpolation, Texture Filtering on Force Nearest, per-pixel transparency, resolution — except the stretch, which follows Dojo's own mode, and the whole [input] section (both controllers on ports A and B), plus the controller mapping files. Whatever you change in those options in the system Flycast also applies to Dojo.

Checked on a virtual screen with package 2.1.45: download, repair, the lobby open, FBNeo opening in Wine, Alt+Tab and Ctrl+W; and, on an Xorg that accepts new modes, the mode switch — a game requested through Fightcade's own fcade:// takes the screen to 320x240 in 1 s, Flycast to 640x240 in full screen, and the lobby comes back on close. On the cabinet's 15 kHz tube, a CPS game in FBNeo opened in its native 384x224, pixel for pixel. Not checked: an online match (needs an account).

ROMs, BIOS and artwork

The folders in ~

ROMs in ~/roms. The collection lives in /home/fliperos/roms (\\fliperos\roms over the network), with one folder per emulator — mame, ps2, dreamcast (Flycast's discs), naomi, naomi2 and atomiswave (Flycast's arcades, one system per folder: in the frontends each one is a system, with its own platform in the Scraper), model3, dolphin, openbor/Paks, hypseus, model2 (a link to the emulator's folder in Wine) — and one per RetroArch core in ~/roms/retroarch/<core> (every core RetroArch knows: those in the core info files, ~330), each one with an _info.txt naming the system and the extensions. fliperos-roms creates whatever is missing at every login, so a new core from the Online Updater gets a folder. RetroArch opens its file browser in ~/roms, GroovyMAME looks in ~/roms/mame, Flycast in ~/roms/dreamcast, naomi, naomi2 and atomiswave (Dreamcast.ContentPath) and PCSX2 lists ~/roms/ps2 (if no folder has been chosen yet). /opt/fliperos/roms became a link to ~/roms: the old paths still work, and an earlier installation had its ROMs moved there (only renamed, without overwriting anything).

BIOS, artwork and settings. Next to ~/roms, fliperos-roms creates (at every login, only what is missing, each folder with its own _info.txt):

  • ~/bios (\\fliperos\bios) — RetroArch's system folder (system_directory): the loose files in it and the subfolders the cores look for. ~/bios/dc is the one for Flycast and the Flycast core (dc_boot.bin, dc_flash.bin, naomi.zip, awbios.zip...; Dreamcast.BiosPath in emu.cfg and FLYCAST_BIOS_PATH in fliperos-x11-run), ~/bios/ps2 the one for PCSX2 (its bios folder, ~/.config/PCSX2/bios, becomes a link there, with whatever it already had) and ~/bios/mame is in GroovyMAME's rompath (BIOS and devices such as neogeo.zip and qsound.zip; they still work from ~/roms/mame). /opt/fliperos/bios became a link to ~/bios. FliperOS ships no BIOS: everyone puts in their own.
  • ~/media (\\fliperos\media) — the frontends' artwork and text, one folder per type and, inside, the same folders as ~/roms (mame, model2, naomi, ps2, retroarch/snes9x...), so the art of a games folder is in the folder of the same name: snap (screenshots), preview (videos), logo (wheel), box (covers and flyers), marquee and texto (each game's description, .txt). Setup > Games > Scraper writes there, and the frontends read from there: Attract-Mode through the emulator's artwork lines, ES-DE through MediaDirectory, Pegasus through metadata.pegasus.txt and GroovyMAME's game list through mame.ini (snapshot_directory) and ui.ini (covers_directory, flyers_directory, marquees_directory, logos_directory). Artwork placed by hand with the ROM name (snap/mame/sf2.png for ~/roms/mame/sf2.zip) also shows in Attract-Mode, ES-DE and GroovyMAME. An installation from before this layout, with one folder per scraper platform (snap/arcade, snap/snes), is moved by itself the next time the launcher opens: see Adding games.
  • ~/config (\\fliperos\config) — links to the configuration of each installed emulator and frontend: retroarch, groovymame (/etc/fliperos/mame, with mame.ini and ui.ini), flycast, pcsx2, dolphin, supermodel, openbor (the .cfg files in ~/roms/openbor/Saves), hypseus, model2, attractmode, es-de, pegasus and skyscraper. Each link appears once the program has created its configuration (usually after being opened once); a deleted one comes back at the next login, and one left without a target (the program was removed) goes away.
  • ~/splashscreen (\\fliperos\splashscreen) — the boot splash themes (Plymouth), one folder per theme with its .plymouth file. The bundled ones (fliperos, fliperos-text, cogwheel, into-it and windoze95) are copied there when missing and follow the new versions of the image while left untouched (a hidden .bundled file keeps the copy's checksum); to change one, copy its folder with a new name. Setup > System > Splash screen previews them and sets the one used at boot (Installed system). More themes: Plymouth Themes on Gnome-look.

Custom folders

Setup > Games > Folders shows the whole structure: ~/roms, ~/bios, ~/media and ~/splashscreen and, inside them, the folder of each emulator (~/roms/mame, ~/roms/model2), of each RetroArch core (~/roms/retroarch/mame2010), of each Fightcade emulator, of each BIOS subfolder and of each type of art (~/media/snap, and inside it the same folders as ~/roms: ~/media/snap/mame, ~/media/snap/model2, ~/media/snap/retroarch/mame2010). Any of them can have its own custom folder: another folder used in its place, for when the ROMs, the art or the BIOS are on external storage (a USB disk, a second drive; the disks are mounted by themselves in /media/fliperos/<label>, where the folder browser opens). So the MAME 2010 sets can be on one disk, the Model 2 videos on another (~/media/preview/model2) and the Model 2 sets somewhere else. Each line says whether the folder has a custom folder, or how many of the folders inside it have one. ~/config is left out: it only holds links.

  • Link this to another folder — the folder becomes a link to the chosen one and the folder used until then is renamed next to it, with old- in the name and everything in it: ~/old-roms, ~/roms/old-mame, ~/roms/retroarch/old-mame2010, ~/media/old-snap. Nothing is copied or deleted. fliperos-roms then creates in the custom folder what is missing, and the default frontend reads the game folders again. Everything that looks in the folder — the emulators, the frontends, the Scraper, the network shares — goes through the link; the old- folders are not listed as systems. On a folder that already has a custom folder, Change the custom folder only swaps the link.
  • Unlink the custom folder — the link goes away and the old- folder gets its name back; if it was deleted, a new empty folder is created. Nothing is deleted from the custom folder.

Nothing about this is saved in a file: the state is what is on the disk, so a link made or removed by hand (SSH, the desktop) counts the same. A link you made yourself shows as a custom folder; a link whose disk is not connected shows as not found, and at login that folder is left alone while the others are created as usual; a link deleted by hand brings the old- folder back at the next login (or when Setup > Games > Folders is opened, also when an empty folder had already been created in its place). A folder with files in it is never removed to make room: if there is already an old-mame when a custom folder is added, the current one is kept as old2-mame. On an installation from before SM2-Emu, ~/roms/model2 was a link to the folder of the old Model 2 Emulator (Wine): the games in it move to a plain ~/roms/model2, and that folder is deleted (config/fliperos-roms). A file that could not be moved keeps the old folder, with it.

MAME ROM Cleaner

In Setup > Games > Cleaner, with the MAME CHD Cleaner. From a folder with a MAME romset, it copies (or moves) into the emulator's folder the games that pass the filters, with the parent, the BIOS and the devices they need. It works like ROMLister: it joins the XML of the romset's MAME version with catver.ini (categories and adult games), nplayers.ini (how two people play) and Controls.dat's controls.xml (the buttons the game really uses), and filters by what the cabinet has. For the Naomi, Naomi 2 and Atomiswave games the controls and the buttons come from Flycast's own game table instead: MAME's XML gives nearly all of them the board's generic panel (an 8-way joystick and 6 buttons), steering wheel and light gun games included, so with only joysticks marked in Controls those games no longer pass. Where the romset is: in a folder on this machine (disk, USB stick; the menu's own folder browser: Enter opens, .. goes back, Use this folder chooses) or in a network shared folder (Windows, NAS: the Setup asks for the computer, the user and the password and lists its shared folders; it is mounted read-only at /mnt/network with cifs-utils, the password goes in a file only root can read, never on the command line, and is only kept, in /etc/fliperos/netshare.cred, if you ask — lib/netshare.sh). That way the whole romset stays on the home computer and the cabinet takes only what it uses. For which emulator: GroovyMAME (the XML is groovymame -listxml; games go to ~/roms/mame and BIOS and devices to ~/bios/mame, which is also in the rompath: outside the games folder they don't show as games in the frontends), Flycast (the same romset and the same XML, only its systems: Naomi, Naomi 2 and Atomiswave, each in its own folder, ~/roms/naomi, ~/roms/naomi2 and ~/roms/atomiswave, with the BIOS — naomi.zip, naomi2.zip, awbios.zip and the special ones — in ~/bios/dc and without MAME's devices, which Flycast doesn't use; a set's system is that of the first BIOS in the romof chain), SM2-Emu and Supermodel (the same romset and XML, only the Sega Model 2 games — the model2 driver, Model 2/2A/2B/2C — into ~/roms/model2, or the Model 3 games — the model3 driver — into ~/roms/model3; neither uses BIOS, and of MAME's devices only SM2-Emu's I/O boards go along, model1io.zip and model1io2.zip, which Daytona USA, Desert Tank and Virtua Cop need beside their zip and which the Model 2 game list hides; as in Flycast, MAME's emulation status doesn't filter; a folder with model2, model3 or supermodel in its name already suggests it), MAME 2010 / MAME 3D (0.139, the one in RetroArch's mame2010 core, which runs the PlayStation-based (PSX) arcade games well — ZN-1/ZN-2, Namco System 11/12, Konami GV/GQ, Taito G-NET: Tekken 3, Soul Calibur, Street Fighter EX —, so its filter starts with the PlayStation-based hardware preset; a folder with 2010 or MAME 3D in its name already suggests it; the XML ships in the image at /usr/local/share/fliperos/mame2010.xml.xz, pinned by commit and sha256; games and BIOS in ~/roms/retroarch/mame2010, where the core looks for them) or another version (an XML from mame -listxml). What was read from the XML stays in /var/cache/fliperos (a minute with GroovyMAME's; the next read takes a second). The parameters screen shows each parameter with its current choice; Enter opens its options — choose one (radio, (*)) or check several (checkbox, [x]: Space or button 3 toggles, a checks all, Enter confirms) — and returns to the screen with the choice made. The parameters: games (coin-op machines only, or everything), emulation (working, with imperfect, or everything), categories (the genres of catver.ini, with how many sets each has; slot machines, pinball, computers and consoles start unchecked; Mahjong, which catver.ini keeps inside Tabletop, is a category of its own and also starts unchecked, in every preset but Everything), adult games (* Mature * in catver.ini), players, play mode (from nplayers.ini: single player, two or more alternating, two or more simultaneous, not stated), buttons (those of controls.xml when it knows the game — Metal Slug has 4 in MAME and uses 3 —, otherwise MAME's), panel controls (8, 4 and 2-way joystick, two joysticks per player, trackball, spinner, paddle, rotary, analog, pedals, light gun, keyboard, mahjong; a buttons-only game always passes; the 0.139 types, joy8way, vjoy2way, doublejoy8way, and the current ones, joy and doublejoy with ways), screen (horizontal, vertical), resolution (low, up to 288 lines, the only progressive one on a 15 kHz tube; medium; high — from the XML's pixclock/htotal or, without them, from the lines), clones (the best version of each game, parents only, or all) and the preferred region, bootlegs, prototypes, decades, vector games, games with CHD (keep, remove, or only those), board (Neo-Geo, CPS-1, CPS-2, CPS-3, the PlayStation-based ones — ZN-1/ZN-2, Namco System 10/11/12, Konami GV/GQ/573/Twinkle, Taito G-NET, from the Sony CPU in the XML — the Midway Mortal Kombat boards, Sega Model 2, Sega Model 3, Sega Naomi, Sega Naomi 2 and Sammy Atomiswave; with no board checked every board goes, except Naomi, Naomi 2 and Atomiswave, which run better in Flycast and only go to GroovyMAME when checked). The three data files ship in the image, in /usr/local/share/fliperos/romclean (config/romclean in the repository): catver.ini 0.289 from progetto-SNAPS, nplayers.ini 0.278 from NPlayers (CC BY-SA 3.0) and controls.xml 0.141.1 from Controls.dat. To use newer ones, or the ones that came with your romset, put them with the romset: the Setup looks first in its folder, in the one above and in the folders subfolder (the MAME UI's), and only then in the system folder, which an update rewrites. The screen says which ones it found and their version (Data files); without one of them, its parameters don't show. Presets: Joystick cabinet (arcade only, up to 2 players and 6 buttons, joysticks, one version of each game, no bootlegs, prototypes or adult games), Everything that works, PlayStation-based hardware (the MAME 3D games) and Everything. Whatever the chosen games use goes along: the parent (in a merged romset the clone is in its zip), the BIOS and the devices with ROMs (romof, cloneof, device_ref), also those of the games already at the destination. Before touching anything, the summary (how many go and their size, the free space, how many aren't in the XML, which are left alone) and the list; the copy shows its progress, each file arrives as .part and only then gets its name (an interrupted copy never passes as complete), whatever is already at the destination with the same size stays — repeating resumes where it stopped — and what arrives is owned by the owner of the emulator's folder. Move (only from a writable folder) takes the files out of the romset and then asks whether to delete what's left; Flycast's sets never enter that list, so the same romset can be cleaned for Flycast afterwards — except in a MAME 2010 romset, which Flycast can't read: there the Naomi and Atomiswave sets the filter leaves out are deleted like the others, instead of staying in the core's folder where the frontend would list games MAME 0.139 doesn't run (config/fliperos-romclean, lib/romclean.sh).

MAME CHD Cleaner

CHDs usually come in a separate, huge collection. This item looks at the games already in the emulator's ROM folder (~/roms/mame; for Flycast, ~/roms/naomi and ~/roms/naomi2; for MAME 2010 / MAME 3D, the core's folder), checks in the MAME XML which ones use a disk and copies, from the CHD folder (one subfolder per game, on this machine or in a network folder), only theirs, into the ROM folder (<folder>/<game>/<disk>.chd, where MAME and Flycast look for them). A clone's disk that is the same as the parent's comes from the parent's folder; a disk without a dump doesn't count. From each folder only the disks those games use are taken: in a merged collection the parent's folder also holds the disks of every other version, and they stay behind (the Downloader does the same, so they are never downloaded). The screen shows how many games use CHDs, how many folders were found and their size, the free space and the list of what wasn't found; you can include the clones inside the zips (merged romset) and change the ROM folder. The copy shows its progress and resumes where it stopped; from a writable folder you can also move (fliperos-romclean chds).

Downloader

Setup > Games > Downloader gathers what downloads games: ROM/CHD MAME torrent, Free games and Download BIOS. The torrent screen, in order: the Filter (the same parameters screen as the ROM Cleaner, for GroovyMAME, Flycast, SM2-Emu, Supermodel or MAME 2010 / MAME 3D (the PlayStation-based arcade games), saved apart, only for the torrent; until one is saved, the Joystick cabinet preset), the ROM set magnet link (required), the CHD set magnet link (optional; both come filled in with the links shipped in the image, MAME 0.289 ROMs and MAME 0.288 CHDs, both merged, from /usr/local/share/fliperos/downloader-magnets: Enter uses them, or paste another), the Download folder (the folder browser; by default the emulator's, ~/roms/mame with the BIOS in ~/bios/mame — elsewhere the BIOS go with the games; for Flycast, the folder of naomi, naomi2 and atomiswave) and Start the download, which needs the ROM link — the CHDs are those of the games the romset brings. It downloads only what the filter chooses — the games, with their parents, BIOS and devices, and the CHDs of those games — never the whole set; the Setup says so when a link is pasted. Before marking anything, Start checks that the whole download fits on the disk; if not, it says how much is needed and how much is free. Where the sets are in the torrent is found from the file list: at its top or in any subfolder (MAME 0.289/ROMs (merged)/, next to Software Lists, readmes or extras) — the folder with the most .zip/.7z sets, or, for CHDs, with the most <game>/<disk>.chd folders; the Setup shows which folder and how many sets, and the rest of the torrent is never downloaded. A torrent without any (the CHD link pasted as the romset) is refused. Transmission does the download: its own transmission-daemon (fliperos-transmission.service, as the fliperos user, at low CPU and disk priority, RPC only on 127.0.0.1:9092; the stock transmission-daemon.service is masked). The link is added running (paused, Transmission doesn't look for peers) and, as soon as the file list arrives, stopped with no file marked; the list goes through fliperos-romclean (scan --names, chds --names --with), only the chosen files are marked, and what is already in the emulator folders isn't downloaded again. The terminal shows a progress bar (size, peers, speed, time left); any key returns to the menu and the download goes on, even with a game open. When a torrent ends, each file goes to the download folder (the BIOS to ~/bios/mame, the CHDs in <game>/) and the torrent leaves Transmission without seeding. When the filter changes (or a new MAME version), with links set up, the Setup says how many ROMs and CHDs it adds (and their size) and how many it deletes, with the list, and asks before applying; only what the downloader brought is ever deleted, never files put there by hand. Removing a link can also delete what it brought. A parent, BIOS or device that a game still in the folders uses (the ROM folders of the filter and GroovyMAME's rompath) is never deleted: neogeo.zip stays while there is an MVS game, even one copied by the ROM Cleaner (fliperos-romclean uses). State in /var/lib/fliperos/downloader (config/fliperos-downloader, lib/downloader.sh). Afterwards, AttractPlus > ROM List updates the game names in the frontend.

Free games

Setup > Games > Downloader > Free games downloads open-source homebrew into the RetroArch core folders (~/roms/retroarch/<core>), so the frontends don't start empty and to test video, sound and controls without anyone's ROMs: the 240p Test Suites for NES, Game Boy and GBA, Thwaite, Nova the Squirrel, Libbet and the Magic Floor and µCity. Each file comes from the project's own releases page, with a pinned sha256 and the license in the list (GPL and zlib, checked in each one's repository); only those for installed cores, and what is already there stays. The image ships nobody's ROMs or BIOS (config/fliperos-freeroms, lib/freeroms.sh).

Download BIOS

Setup > Games > Downloader > Download BIOS brings the BIOS that the games in your folders need and ~/bios doesn't have yet. It looks at every folder of ~/roms that has files:

  • RetroArch cores (~/roms/retroarch/<core>): the firmware listed in the core's own .info file (PCSX ReARMed asks for scph5501.bin, Flycast for dc/dc_boot.bin, FBNeo for fbneo/neogeo.zip), into ~/bios at the path the core asks for. The optional ones come too: without them a core falls back to an imitation BIOS (HLE) or doesn't open part of its games (Sega CD, Famicom Disk System);
  • MAME sets (~/roms/mame, naomi, naomi2, atomiswave, and the arcade cores): the BIOS and devices those sets use, from the MAME XML (fliperos-romclean uses): neogeo.zip, qsound.zip… go to ~/bios/mame, the Naomi and Atomiswave ones to ~/bios/dc, FBNeo's to ~/bios/fbneo, and those of the other MAME cores next to their games;
  • PCSX2 (~/roms/ps2) and Flycast (~/roms/dreamcast): their BIOS in ~/bios/ps2 and ~/bios/dc.

The files come from a reference BIOS pack, the Batocera v36 full BIOS pack on the Internet Archive (/usr/local/share/fliperos/bios-pack). Its torrent is only read, never downloaded: it gives the archive item and the name of its 1.1 GB .zip, and the Internet Archive serves each file inside that .zip on its own, so only the missing BIOS are downloaded (on the cabinet, 13 files and 22.6 MB for PS1, Naomi, Atomiswave and Neo-Geo games). A BIOS already in place, also one put next to the games, is never downloaded again, and nothing is overwritten. Files over 64 MB (the DSi NAND, the PS3 and Vita updates) are left out.

The check also runs by itself each time the FliperOS menu opens, in the background (fliperos-setup --bios-check), so a game folder copied over the network gets its BIOS without anyone asking. It only runs when a folder of ~/roms or ~/bios changed since the last check (a deleted BIOS comes back); without network it tries again 10 minutes later. Check when the menu opens turns it off (bios_auto=off in fliperos.conf). The screen shows how many were downloaded, how many were already there and which ones aren't in the pack, which you put in ~/bios yourself. The pack is Batocera 36's: a newer MAME may want a BIOS file it doesn't have yet. State in /var/lib/fliperos/bios (config/fliperos-bios, lib/downloader.sh).

Scraper

Setup > Games > Scraper has three items: Scrape the games, Scraper provider and Art packs by torrent.

Scraper provider picks where the art and the information come from, and keeps the account of the providers that ask for one, the way ES-DE and Arcadeflow keep the ScreenScraper login in their options:

Provider Has Account
ScreenScraper every system user and password, optional (a free account raises the daily limits)
TheGamesDB consoles and computers none
ArcadeDB arcade games none
progetto-SNAPS arcade screenshots only none
MobyGames every system your API key, required
IGDB consoles and computers a Twitch client ID and secret, required
OpenRetro Amiga and old computers none

The choice is saved in fliperos.conf (scraper_source) and the account in /etc/fliperos/scraper-accounts, a file only root reads; it is what Skyscraper gets in -u. A provider that only works with an account doesn't scrape until it has one.

Art packs by torrent brings logos and videos from torrents, without an account and without the per-day limits of the providers. The screen comes filled with packs from the Internet Archive: logos and videos for MAME (also used by the RetroArch arcade cores), Naomi and Naomi 2, Atomiswave, Model 2 and Model 3. Each pack belongs to some folders of ~/roms, and it only downloads when those folders have ROMs, and only the files of the games that are in them: the torrent's file list is compared with the names of the ROMs, and the rest of the torrent is never downloaded. A game that already has that art in ~/media is skipped. Where a pack keeps the files inside .zip archives by letter (the MAME videos), only the archives of the letters your games start with are downloaded, and only your games' files come out of them. The download is done by the same Transmission service as the Downloader, in the background, even with a game open; when a torrent ends, each file goes to ~/media/logo/<games folder> or ~/media/preview/<games folder> with the name of the ROM, and the torrent is removed. Add a torrent takes a pack of your own (a magnet link or the address of a .torrent, the kind of art and the folder it is for), and the link of a pack that comes filled in can be changed. The packs are in config/fliperos-media-torrents and the program is /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-mediapacks.

Scrape the games — covers, screenshots, logos, videos and information for each ROM, with Skyscraper (the same one GroovyArcade packages), from the one chosen in Scraper provider. What to fetch is a checklist: screenshots (snap), logos, covers (box), marquees, descriptions (texto) and videos (preview, unchecked at first: they take much more space). Skyscraper only downloads the checked types (--flags noscreenshots,nocovers,nowheels,nomarquees and videos) and only exports those; the choice is remembered. An artwork type the folder didn't have yet makes it fetch again the games already in the cache (--refresh): without that Skyscraper doesn't go back to them. The folders with games (each one's _info.txt doesn't count) that FliperOS knows how to open are included: ~/roms/mame, dreamcast, ps2, dolphin and the RetroArch core folders (~/roms/retroarch/<core>, by the platform of the systemid in the core's .info). Artwork and text go to ~/media/<type>/<system> (ROMs, BIOS and artwork): Skyscraper writes each type into a subfolder with a fixed name (screenshots, covers, wheels, marquees, videos), and for it ~/media/.skyscraper/<system> has links with those names to snap, box, logo, marquee and preview; the descriptions come out of the generated list into ~/media/texto. The list goes to the frontend. With GroovyMAME as the default launcher, the MAME folder only gets the artwork: mame.ini and ui.ini already look in ~/media/<type>/arcade (the first snapshot_directory is still F12's). In ES-DE (FliperOS's EmulationStation) the list goes to ~/ES-DE/gamelists/<system> (Skyscraper's esde format, relative paths) and MediaDirectory is ~/media/.es-de, with one link per ES-DE type (screenshots, covers, marquees = logo, videos) into ~/media. Earlier artwork (~/.mame/scraped/mame, ~/.attract/scraped/<folder>, <ROM folder>/media) moves to ~/media in tools/cabinet-update.sh and when the folder is scraped. The images come out each one as it came (screenshot, cover, logo, marquee; the Setup's artwork.xml), and not composited on top of each other as in Skyscraper's default. Clones: in the MAME folder the Setup asks whether to also fetch the clones of the existing games (groovymame -listclones: mvscu, mvscj...), which in a merged romset are inside the parent's zip and have no file — the scraped folder becomes one of references in ~/.cache/fliperos-scraper (links to the ROMs and a small file per clone). Resuming: Skyscraper only writes its cache at the end of each run, so the games go in batches of 20 (--includefrom) and the work is recorded in /var/lib/fliperos/scraper (the folders still to do, the files of each one already saved, the source and the account, readable by root only); if the machine was switched off or the Setup closed midway, Setup > Games > Scraper offers to resume from the batch where it stopped. In Attract-Mode Plus (the default launcher's, or with no frontend chosen, if it is installed) the Setup creates, like GroovyArcade's efc.sh, any missing emulator in ~/.attract/emulators — with the command that opens the game (fliperos-x11-run groovymame [name], fliperos-kms-run retroarch -L <core>, fliperos-x11-run flycast/pcsx2/dolphin-emu) and the artwork in ~/media/<type>/<system> — and its display in attract.cfg (AdvanceMenu theme); Skyscraper writes the romlist to ~/.attract/romlists and the descriptions to ~/.attract/scraper/<emulator>/overview, a link to ~/media/texto/<system>. In Pegasus, metadata.pegasus.txt lives in the ROM folder itself, pointing to the artwork in ~/media. What already exists (a display, an emulator changed in Attract-Mode) isn't rewritten — the emulator created by the Setup is redone, so the artwork moves to ~/media —, and a system that fails doesn't stop the others.

progetto-SNAPS (no account) is one of the providers, for the arcade folders only (MAME, Naomi, Atomiswave...): the in-game screenshots of progetto-SNAPS by AntoPISA, the same set GroovyArcade users know. /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-snaps follows the site's guidelines: the pack names come from its Snapshots page and each pack goes through its download link, one at a time; the monthly update (pS_snap_upd_N.zip) wins over the full set (pS_snap_fullset_N.zip, about 520 MB, with the images in a 7z). The packs stay in /var/cache/fliperos/snaps (about 1 GB with the 7z unpacked), so scraping another folder later downloads nothing again, and a newer version replaces the older one. Only the screenshots of the games in the folder are copied, to ~/media/snap/<games folder> (~/media/snap/mame); one that is already there stays. The site doesn't host logos, covers or descriptions, so those still come from the other sources; the Attract-Mode Plus romlist and the ES-DE/Pegasus lists are refreshed the same way.

Adding games

A step-by-step guide to putting games, names and artwork into Attract-Mode Plus and EmulationStation (ES-DE), to the emulator command lines (KMS or X11) and to adding a new emulator that opens from any frontend. The folders themselves are described in ROMs, BIOS and artwork; the automatic artwork download is in Scraper.

Copy the games

Each emulator has its folder in ~/roms (/home/fliperos/roms; over the network, \\fliperos\roms). Copy the files as they come: MAME sets stay zipped and are never renamed (the frontends and MAME find a game by its set name, sf2ce.zip).

Folder What goes in it Opens in
mame MAME sets (.zip, .7z) and their CHD folders GroovyMAME
naomi, naomi2, atomiswave MAME sets of these systems Flycast
model3 MAME sets of Model 3 Supermodel
model2 MAME sets of Model 2 SM2-Emu
dreamcast .gdi, .cdi, .chd Flycast
ps2 .iso, .chd, .cso PCSX2
dolphin GameCube and Wii (.iso, .rvz, .wbfs) Dolphin
retroarch/<core> Whatever that core opens (the _info.txt in each folder lists the extensions) RetroArch

BIOS files go in ~/bios (\\fliperos\bios): ~/bios/mame for MAME (neogeo.zip, qsound.zip), ~/bios/dc for Flycast (naomi.zip, awbios.zip, dc_boot.bin), ~/bios/ps2 for PCSX2 and the folder itself for the RetroArch cores. A BIOS left in a games folder shows up as a game in the frontend.

Make the frontend see the folder

A folder with games becomes a system in the frontend when you pick the frontend in Setup > Games > Frontend. For ES-DE and Pegasus, pick it again (even if it already is the default) every time a folder gets its first games: the Setup only creates what is missing, so nothing you changed is lost. Attract-Mode Plus gets the new folder by itself the next time it opens.

  • Attract-Mode Plus: for each folder the Setup creates an emulator (~/.attract/emulators/<name>.cfg, with the command that opens the game and where the artwork is), a display in ~/.attract/config/displays.cfg (~/.attract/attract.cfg before Attract-Mode Plus first runs), and a game list (romlist, ~/.attract/romlists/<name>.txt) built from the files in the folder.

The display uses the Arcadeflow theme, the default in FliperOS. It doesn't come with FliperOS, because its repository has no license that allows redistributing it: when you pick Attract-Mode Plus, Setup > Games > Frontend downloads version 17.7 from its GitHub (55 MB) to ~/.attract/layouts/Arcadeflow. Every display is on Arcadeflow by default: each time the launcher opens, the displays on Basic or BasicPlus (the layout Attract-Mode Plus gives the displays it creates itself) move to it; a display you put on another layout (Attract-Mode's menu, Tab > Displays) stays there. The theme keeps its options and its game database in its own folder; FliperOS turns off one of its options, the check for new versions (it waits for an answer on the keyboard). The select button keeps the theme's default: the first press opens the context menu over the game (information, favourite, tags) and the second one opens the game; to open the game at the first press, set Context menu button: None in the theme's options. Clones are grouped, Attract-Mode Plus's default (Group Clones, in Tab > General): the list has one tile per family, and opening a game that has clones shows the list of its clones — pick one to play, or press back to return to the whole list. FliperOS adds one line to the theme for this: Attract-Mode Plus swaps the list for the clones instead of starting the game, and the theme, which had already cleared the screen for the game, was left showing only its background, with the clone list there but unseen until another press. To have every clone as a tile of its own, turn Group Clones off. FliperOS also turns off the theme's Game not available indicator: it marks with a red cross, and refuses to open, a game with no file of its own in the folder, and in a merged romset a clone lives inside its parent's zip (GroovyMAME opens it by name). Arcadeflow is drawn for high-resolution screens and uses shaders on every frame: on a 15 kHz tube (320x240) turn on Small screen and Low Spec mode in its options, or go back to Basic if the text is too small. On a 15 kHz tube in a wide-pixel mode such as 640x240 (8:3 in pixels, shown as 4:3 by the tube) FliperOS makes the theme draw in 4:3 at the same height (320x240, the theme's own Resolution W x H option) and stretch to the width of the mode (200%), like the emulators do; left alone it arranges itself for an 8:3 screen and everything comes out twice as wide. This is set each time the launcher opens, from the mode the console is in, and undone in a 4:3 mode; a size you typed there yourself in a 4:3 mode is kept.

Without Arcadeflow (no network at that moment; pick the frontend again to retry) the display uses the Basic layout, FliperOS's version of the one that comes with Attract-Mode Plus (config/attractplus-layouts/Basic, in ~/.attract/layouts, where it wins over the packaged one): the same look, drawn at the screen's real resolution with slightly larger letters. The original draws at a fixed 640x480 and Attract-Mode scales it, which blurs the text at 320x240 and 640x240. Every display also gets the default filters: All Games, Most Played (the 25 most played), by genre (Category, from catver.ini: Driving, Fighting, Shooting...), by manufacturer (© Capcom, © SNK, © Sega...) and by release year. The Setup adds them to any display that has no filter, including the ones Attract-Mode itself created; a display with filters of its own is left as it is. - ES-DE: the Setup writes every system to ~/ES-DE/custom_systems/es_systems.xml (folder, extensions, command and theme). ES-DE reads the folders itself each time it starts.

New games in a folder that is already a system. ES-DE finds them the next time it opens (or right away with Main menu > Utilities > Rescan ROM directory). Attract-Mode Plus doesn't look at the folder: its list is the romlist. FliperOS brings the romlist up to date each time the launcher opens (and in Setup > Games > Frontend, Folders and AttractPlus ROM List): a file with one of the emulator's extensions that is not in the list goes in, under its file name, and a game whose file is gone leaves; the other lines stay as they are, with the title and year the Scraper wrote. A folder with its first games gets its emulator and display the same way. A file still being copied (.part) only enters when the copy ends. The exception is an arcade list with real game names: it leaves the BIOS sets out and has the clones that live inside the parent's zip, so it is only redone in Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List. To rebuild a list yourself:

  • Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List (arcade folders, below);
  • Setup > Games > Scraper (any folder; it also fetches the artwork);
  • in Attract-Mode itself: Tab opens the configuration, Emulators > (the emulator) > Generate Collection/Rom List;
  • or delete ~/.attract/romlists/<name>.txt and pick the frontend again in Setup > Games > Frontend.

Real game names

A game list made only from the files shows the file name: mvsc2u instead of Marvel Vs. Capcom 2 New Age of Heroes.

Attract-Mode Plus. The name is the second field of each line in the romlist (Name;Title;Emulator;CloneOf;Year;Manufacturer;...). Two ways to fill it in:

  • Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List ? for the arcade folders (mame, naomi, naomi2, atomiswave, model2, model3 and the RetroArch arcade cores: mame2010, mame2003_plus, fbneo, hbmame...). No internet needed: the name, year, manufacturer, players, controls and emulation status of each game come from GroovyMAME's game list (groovymame -listxml), and from the MAME 0.139 list for the mame2010 folder. Clones and bootlegs come in with their own names and with their parent in the CloneOf field. In the MAME folder the list also gets the clones that only exist inside the parent's zip (a merged romset), because GroovyMAME opens a game by its name and finds the clone there. The other emulators open a file, so in their folders only the clones that have their own file are listed. A set that isn't in MAME's list keeps its file name, and BIOS sets are left out. The category comes from the catver.ini that ships in the image (/usr/local/share/fliperos/romclean), or from one of your own in ~/roms/mame. The Setup also creates an Arcade display with all those folders together. The first run reads GroovyMAME's list (about a minute); the next ones take a second.
  • Setup > Games > Scraper ? for every folder, with names and descriptions from ScreenScraper, ArcadeDB or TheGamesDB, plus the artwork.

Each of the two rewrites the list of the folders it works on, so whichever ran last wins. The artwork is found by the set name, so it is kept either way.

Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List also goes through every other folder with games (consoles, the arcade folders you left unmarked) and gives it the emulator, the display and the game list it is missing, so a system whose display was removed comes back. At the end it cleans up: a romlist with no game in it is deleted, with the displays that show it, and so is an emulator left without a romlist. Those are mostly the ones Attract-Mode Plus creates by itself on its first run, one for each program it finds (mame, flycast, pcsx2...), pointing at its own ROM folders instead of FliperOS's — empty displays next to the Setup's. Arcadeflow's collections (AF ...) are left alone, and nothing is removed while Attract-Mode Plus is open.

Arcadeflow keeps its own copy of the list. The theme reads the title, year, manufacturer and genre of each game once, into ~/.attract/romlists/<name>.db1, and after that only adds the games that are missing from it. So when a romlist is rebuilt (by the ROM List, the Scraper or Attract-Mode), FliperOS empties the .db1 that is older than its romlist, before Attract-Mode Plus opens: the theme reads the whole list again the next time the display opens. Favourites, play counts and last played (<name>.db2) are kept; what Arcadeflow's own scraper had fetched for that list is not.

A game scraped inside Arcadeflow keeps its romlist data. The theme's scraper replaces the title, year, manufacturer, genre, players, rotation, control and button count with what ArcadeDB answers, and writes them empty when it gets no answer — the game then shows no name and no players/control/buttons indicators at the top. Before Attract-Mode Plus opens, FliperOS puts the romlist's value back into every one of those fields that is empty in the .db1; what the scraper did fill is kept.

The logo of each display. In its displays menu Arcadeflow shows the logo and the picture of a system (system_images, system_bgs) when the display's name is one of the names it knows for that system, listed in the theme's data_systems.txt. It knows Model 2, Naomi and MAME, but not Supermodel or Arcade (various) (MAME 2010 (0.139)), the names FliperOS gives the displays of those folders. Before Attract-Mode Plus opens, FliperOS adds the name of each of its displays to the system it belongs to in that file (fliperos-arcadeflow systems): Sega Model 3 for the Supermodel folder, GameCube for Dolphin, FBNeo for its core, and for the other cores the platform of the folder (PlayStation, SNES...). A RetroArch MAME core is a system of its own, named after the core without its version, so the MAME 2010 display is MAME 2010 and not a second MAME next to GroovyMAME's. In the menu the logo of a system is a glyph of the theme's font (fonts/font_Roboto-Allcaps-EXT4X.ttf), numbered by the position of the system in data_systems.txt; a system added at the end has no glyph and showed as a dot. So FliperOS adds one to the font: the MAME logo with "2010" beside it, tilted (the theme's own font is kept next to it as .fliperos-orig). Its picture is the theme's MAME one with "2010" written across it. Both are made on the machine by fliperos-arcadeflow-art, with fontTools and Pillow, which are installed from apt together with the theme; without fontTools the display stays a plain MAME, and without Pillow it uses the plain MAME picture. What FliperOS added is listed in the theme's fliperos_systems.txt and taken out again when a display goes away. The logo under each game's title is another thing: it follows the system line of the emulator's .cfg, which is arcade in every arcade folder.

ES-DE. In systems with the arcade platform (mame, model2, model3 and the RetroArch MAME cores), ES-DE already shows the full name of each MAME set from its own list. Everything else shows the file name until it is scraped: Setup > Games > Scraper writes the names, descriptions and artwork to ~/ES-DE/gamelists/<system>/gamelist.xml. ES-DE's own scraper (Main menu > Scraper) also works and saves to the same places.

Artwork

All artwork lives in ~/media (\\fliperos\media), one folder per type and, inside it, the same folders as ~/roms. The file name is the ROM name without the extension: the screenshot of ~/roms/mame/sf2.zip is ~/media/snap/mame/sf2.png, and the video of ~/roms/retroarch/mame2010/tekken3.zip is ~/media/preview/retroarch/mame2010/tekken3.mp4. Setup > Games > Scraper fills these folders, but you can also copy images and videos there yourself (from a MAME artwork pack, for example).

~/media folder What it holds Attract-Mode Plus (artwork line) ES-DE
snap Screenshots (.png) snap screenshot
preview Videos (.mp4) snap (videos sit in the same line) video
logo Logos, wheel art (.png) wheel marquee

marquee Cabinet marquees (.png) marquee ?

Each games folder has its own art folder in every type, with the same name: mame, model2, model3, naomi, naomi2, atomiswave, dreamcast, ps2, dolphin and retroarch/<core>. So two folders with the same game (Daytona USA in mame and in model2) can each have their own video, and each art folder can be on another disk (Setup > Games > Folders).

Shared arcade artwork. In Attract-Mode Plus, the other arcade folders (Model 2, Model 3, Naomi, Atomiswave, the MAME and FBNeo cores...) look in their own folder first and then in the mame one: the artwork of a set you have for MAME also shows for the same set in the other emulators. You can see it in the emulator's .cfg:

artwork    snap            /home/fliperos/media/snap/naomi;/home/fliperos/media/preview/naomi;/home/fliperos/media/snap/mame;/home/fliperos/media/preview/mame
artwork    wheel           /home/fliperos/media/logo/naomi;/home/fliperos/media/logo/mame

Coming from the layout by platform. Before, ~/media/<type> had one folder per scraper platform (arcade, snes, psx...), shared by every games folder of that platform. FliperOS moves them the next time the launcher opens: arcade goes to mame, and the folder of another platform to the first games folder of that platform (snes to retroarch/snes9x). The other games folders of the platform get the art of the games they have (a hard link, no extra disk space). A platform folder that was a custom folder becomes the same custom folder in each of its games folders, and nothing is written inside a custom folder. A platform folder whose games folder is not there at that moment (a disk that is off) waits for the next time.

ES-DE reads the artwork from ~/media/.es-de/<system>/<type>, where each type is a link to the right folder in ~/media. It only looks in the system's own folder.

Emulator command lines: KMS or X11

What KMS and X11 are

Linux has two ways to put a program on the screen, and FliperOS uses both.

  • KMS (Kernel Mode Setting, through the kernel's DRM driver) means the program draws directly on the video card, without any window system. It is the lightest and fastest path, with the least input lag, and the program changes the video mode itself. RetroArch (with its CRT SwitchRes, one mode per game) and the frontends (Attract-Mode Plus, ES-DE, Pegasus) run like this. The FliperOS wrapper is fliperos-kms-run: it starts the program on the console and, for RetroArch, adds the FliperOS settings (video driver, button mapping) on top of the user's.
  • X11 (Xorg, the classic Linux window system) is for programs that only know how to open a window: Flycast, PCSX2, Dolphin, Supermodel, SM2-Emu (Model 2), Hypseus, GroovyMAME. The FliperOS wrapper is fliperos-x11-run: it starts an Xorg just for that program, has Switchres create a video mode for the monitor chosen in Video Setup (640x240 at 15 kHz for the 480-line systems, so nothing flickers), opens the program full screen and closes Xorg when the program exits. Nothing else runs in that X: no desktop, no panel.

Rule of thumb: RetroArch, in KMS; any other emulator, in X11. If a program says "Cannot open display" or doesn't start in KMS, it needs X11. Both wrappers must be started from the console, as the frontends do: inside the LXDE desktop (which already is an X session), run the emulator directly.

The commands, as a frontend calls them:

# KMS: RetroArch with a core
/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-kms-run retroarch -L /opt/fliperos/retroarch/cores/fbneo_libretro.so "/home/fliperos/roms/retroarch/fbneo/sf2.zip"

# X11: the mode comes from /etc/fliperos/emulator-modes.conf
/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run flycast "/home/fliperos/roms/naomi/mvsc2.zip"

# X11 with a mode chosen on the spot (width x height @ Hz)
/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run --mode 640x240@60 pcsx2 "/home/fliperos/roms/ps2/game.iso"

# GroovyMAME opens a game by its set name
/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run groovymame sf2

The X11 video mode of each program is in /etc/fliperos/emulator-modes.conf (one column for 15 kHz, another for 25/31 kHz and LCD; a program not in the table gets 320x240@60). Add a line there for a new program instead of repeating --mode everywhere. More on the modes in Emulators.

Attract-Mode Plus

Each emulator is a file in ~/.attract/emulators/<name>.cfg (in the cabinet's ~/config/attractmode). The lines that matter:

executable           /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run
args                 supermodel "[romfilename]"
workdir              $HOME
rompath              /home/fliperos/roms/model3/
romext               .zip
system               arcade

[romfilename] is the full path of the game file (keep it in quotes: file names have spaces), and [name] is the name without the extension, for emulators that open a game by name (GroovyMAME, Model 2). For a KMS program, executable is /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-kms-run and args starts with the program (retroarch -L <core> "[romfilename]").

To add an emulator of your own: copy a .cfg from the folder with a new name, change those lines, give it a romlist (Emulators > (the emulator) > Generate Collection/Rom List in Attract-Mode's configuration, or Setup > Games > AttractPlus ROM List for an arcade folder) and a display (Displays > Add new display, with that romlist). The Setup rewrites the .cfg files it created itself (the ones whose first line is # Criado pelo FliperOS Setup), to keep the artwork paths up to date: to change the command of one of them and keep your change, delete that first line.

ES-DE

Each system is a <system> block in ~/ES-DE/custom_systems/es_systems.xml:

<system>
  <name>model3</name>
  <fullname>Supermodel</fullname>
  <path>/home/fliperos/roms/model3</path>
  <extension>.zip .ZIP</extension>
  <command label="Supermodel">/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run supermodel %ROM%</command>
  <platform>arcade</platform>
  <theme>model3</theme>
</system>

<platform> is what the scraper searches as (Model 2, Model 3 and the MAME cores are MAME sets, so arcade); <theme> is the system's folder in the ES-DE themes, which gives it its logo and background. The Setup gives Model 2 and Model 3 their own (model2, model3), the RetroArch MAME cores (mame2010, mame2003_plus...) the MAME one (mame), FBNeo fbneo, and the GroovyMAME folder arcade.

%ROM% is the game's path (ES-DE adds the quotes) and %BASENAME% the name without the extension (/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run groovymame %BASENAME%). A system can have more than one <command> with different labels: ES-DE lets you pick one per game (Edit this game's metadata > Alternative emulator). <platform> decides the scraper's platform and <theme> the system's look.

Setup > Games > Frontend rewrites the whole es_systems.xml every time ES-DE is picked, so a system added by hand disappears. Keep a copy of your blocks and paste them back after using Setup > Games > Frontend.

Custom games

The quick way for a game or program that is not a ROM (a PC game through Wine, a native Linux game, your own emulator with one game, a script): Setup > Games > Custom games. Each entry has a group, a name, the command (as you would type it in a terminal) and the video mode: 240p (640x240), 480i (640x480, interlaced on a 15 kHz monitor) or any WIDTHxHEIGHT@HZ. The frontends show each group as a system and each entry as a game in it, and the entry can also be the game that opens at boot (Setup > Games > Frontend).

Behind it, a group is the folder ~/roms/custom/<group> and each entry a script <name>.sh in it, which opens the command in its own Xorg in that mode (fliperos-x11-run --mode). The mode and the command are also in the script's first lines, which is what the Setup reads to edit it. A .sh you put in one of those folders by hand shows up in the frontends too (with the mode and the command empty in the Setup). The Scraper skips these folders: there is no art to look up for a script.

Adding a new emulator

FliperOS sets up the emulators it ships by itself. For any other emulator (an AppImage, a package, something you built), there are four steps. If you follow them, the same command works in Attract-Mode Plus, ES-DE, Pegasus or the console.

Step 1: install it and test it by hand

Put the program where the system finds it: a package (sudo apt install ...), or the binary or AppImage in /usr/local/bin (chmod +x). Then, in the console (Exit to shell in the FliperOS menu), open a game through the wrapper:

/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run myemu "/home/fliperos/roms/myemu/game.bin"

If it opens full screen, plays and returns to the console when you quit it, it will work from any frontend. Use fliperos-kms-run instead only if the program runs without X (most don't; see What KMS and X11 are).

Step 2: make the command frontend-proof

A frontend only knows how to run one command with the game's path and then wait for it to finish. The command must therefore follow a few rules:

  • Absolute paths: start with /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run (or fliperos-kms-run), never just the program name. The frontends don't run a shell with your PATH.
  • The program right after the wrapper: fliperos-x11-run [--mode WxH@HZ] program [options] game. The wrapper accepts a name in the PATH or the full path of an executable.
  • Full screen and no questions: pass the emulator's own options for full screen, for skipping its welcome or update dialogs and for loading the game directly. Nobody should need a mouse.
  • It has to quit and stay in the foreground: the emulator needs a way to exit with the panel or the keyboard (a hotkey or a menu), otherwise you are stuck in it. It also must not hand off to another process and exit: when the program the wrapper started ends, the wrapper closes its X and the frontend comes back.
  • The game path in quotes: file names have spaces. Each frontend writes the path with its own placeholder (table below).

If the emulator needs more than that (environment variables, a config file chosen per game, a cd to its folder, Wine), write a small launcher script and point the frontends to it. The frontend then only passes the game, and every detail stays in one place:

#!/bin/bash
# /usr/local/bin/myemu-run GAME
export MYEMU_BIOS="$HOME/bios/myemu"
cd /opt/myemu || exit 1
exec /opt/myemu/myemu --fullscreen --no-gui "$1"

sudo chmod +x /usr/local/bin/myemu-run
/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run myemu-run "/home/fliperos/roms/myemu/game.bin"

The exec keeps the emulator as the program the wrapper waits for. This is how FliperOS opens SM2-Emu for a frontend (fliperos-model2).

Step 3: the video mode (X11 only)

fliperos-x11-run takes the mode from /etc/fliperos/emulator-modes.conf by the program name (the first word after the wrapper, without its path). Add a line:

# program           15 kHz          25/31 kHz and LCD
myemu-run           320x240@60      640x480@60

Without a line, the program gets 320x240@60. Use 640x240 at 15 kHz for systems that draw 448 or 480 lines, and the system's own refresh rate when it isn't 60 Hz (Model 2 and 3: 57.524).

Step 4: one folder and the command in each frontend

Create the game folder, for example ~/roms/myemu. Setup > Games > Frontend only creates the systems FliperOS knows about, so a new emulator has to be added by hand in each frontend you use. The command is the same everywhere; only the placeholders change:

Frontend Where Game path Name without extension
Attract-Mode Plus args in ~/.attract/emulators/<name>.cfg "[romfilename]" [name]
ES-DE <command> in ~/ES-DE/custom_systems/es_systems.xml %ROM% (ES-DE adds the quotes) %BASENAME%
Pegasus launch: in metadata.pegasus.txt in the game folder "{file.path}" {file.basename}

The same emulator in the three of them:

# Attract-Mode Plus: ~/.attract/emulators/My Emulator.cfg
executable           /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run
args                 myemu-run "[romfilename]"
rompath              /home/fliperos/roms/myemu/
romext               .bin;.zip

<!-- ES-DE: inside <systemList> in ~/ES-DE/custom_systems/es_systems.xml -->
<system>
  <name>myemu</name>
  <fullname>My Emulator</fullname>
  <path>/home/fliperos/roms/myemu</path>
  <extension>.bin .zip</extension>
  <command label="My Emulator">/opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run myemu-run %ROM%</command>
  <platform>arcade</platform>
  <theme>arcade</theme>
</system>

# Pegasus: ~/roms/myemu/metadata.pegasus.txt
collection: My Emulator
extensions: bin, zip
launch: /opt/fliperos/bin/fliperos-x11-run myemu-run "{file.path}"

Then, in Attract-Mode Plus, generate the romlist and add a display (as in Attract-Mode Plus above). In Pegasus, add the folder to the list in ~/.config/pegasus-frontend/game_dirs.txt. For the artwork, use a ~/media/<type>/<platform> folder (Artwork) in the emulator's artwork lines; for ES-DE, use the <platform> that the scraper should use.

What the Setup keeps and what it rewrites. A .cfg you created yourself, without the # Criado pelo FliperOS Setup line, is never touched, and neither is a metadata.pegasus.txt that already exists. Setup > Games > Frontend does rewrite ES-DE's es_systems.xml and Pegasus's game_dirs.txt with only the folders it knows, so add your system again after using it.

Quick checks

  • The system doesn't show up: the folder must have at least one game file (the _info.txt doesn't count) and the frontend must be picked again in Setup > Games > Frontend.
  • The game doesn't open: with debug mode on (Setup > System > Debug mode) the emulator's output shows on the screen; otherwise it is saved in /opt/fliperos/logs. Try the same command in the console (Exit to shell in the FliperOS menu).
  • No artwork: check the name (exactly the ROM name, without the extension, case included) and the platform folder, and in Attract-Mode the emulator's artwork lines.

Controls

Controllers on FliperOS: what the Setup offers in Joysticks and in System > Quirks and how the emulators see each controller. Using the controller in the Setup menus is covered in Installed system.

Light gun (GunCon 2)

FliperOS plays light gun games on the tube with Namco's GunCon 2 (the PlayStation 2 gun, USB 0b9a:016a), with nothing to install. The gun reads the beam of the CRT, which is exactly the screen FliperOS is made for; on an LCD it doesn't aim. What ships with the system:

  • The driver. GunCon 2 has no driver in the mainline kernel. The build compiles beardypig/guncon2 through DKMS for the system's kernel and loads it at boot (/etc/modules-load.d/fliperos-guncon2.conf): plug the gun into USB and it shows up as an input device.
  • The calibration. The raw position the gun reports depends on the tube and on where the cabinet's gun sits, and evdev forgets the range on every reconnection. Setup > Joysticks > Calibrate GunCon 2 measures it on your monitor once (below), and the udev rule 99-fliperos-input.rules reapplies it (fliperos-guncon2-calibrate) every time the gun is plugged in.
  • The menus. The controller-as-keyboard of the Setup menus leaves light guns out, so aiming doesn't move through the menu.
  • The games. In the MAME ROM Cleaner, tell it the cabinet has a Light gun among its controls, and the gun games of the romset are kept along with the others.

Setup > Joysticks

Calibrators and the parallel port (config/fliperos-calibrate, screens/joysticks.sh):

Button mapping (all emulators)

The first question is what is wired to the panel. Keyboard encoder (MAME default keys) is for an encoder that types keys (I-PAC and the like) in MAME's layout: nothing is pressed, only the number of players is asked, and every emulator below gets MAME's default keys for each player (fliperos-buttons keyboard; player 1: arrows, Left Ctrl, Left Alt, Space, Left Shift, Z, X, 1 Start, 5 Coin; player 2: R F D G, A S Q W E, 2, 6; players 3 and 4 have only the stick and buttons 1 to 3, as in MAME). RetroArch's own shortcuts that sit on those keys (Space fast-forward, F fullscreen, K frame advance...) are turned off in buttons.cfg, and Supermodel drops its own default keys, which would be another player's. Controllers (press each control) is the mapping below.

Each player's panel, input by input: up, down, left, right, buttons 1 to 6 in panel order (1 2 3 on top, 4 5 6 below), Start and Coin. The first input pressed tells which controller belongs to the player (on a V-USB panel with two identical devices, one per player); with nothing pressed for 8 seconds the input is skipped; the controller-as-keyboard for the menu is off meanwhile. It is read through SDL (config/fliperos-buttons), and the result goes to both: in RetroArch, each player's binds in /etc/fliperos/retroarch/buttons.cfg, which fliperos-kms-run adds to the config (--appendconfig) on top of the controller's automatic profile, with the buttons in the arcade cores' layout (Y X L / B A R, Start and Select on the coin); in GroovyMAME, the ctrlr ~/.mame/ctrlr/fliperos.cfg (each player's JOYCODEs, together with MAME's default keys) and in mame.ini ctrlr fliperos and joystickprovider sdljoy — with sdlgame, the default, MAME sees the panel through SDL's game controller mapping (A, B, X, Y...), which doesn't follow the button order. It also applies to the other emulators: the device's SDL game controller mapping (GUID), in fight stick layout (X Y RB on top, A B RT below, coin on Back), in /var/lib/fliperos/gamecontrollerdb-user.txt, which fliperos-controllers puts last in gamecontrollerdb.txt (PCSX2, with Pad1/Pad2 on SDL through fliperos-x11-run while PCSX2 has no SDL bind; DuckStation the same way — it points SDL to its own database, but SDL_GAMECONTROLLERCONFIG_FILE takes priority; KOF Final Attack's Ikemen GO, which adds its own external/gamecontrollerdb.txt after SDL starts, so fliperos-kof hands it that database with the FliperOS one last, save/gamecontrollerdb-fliperos.txt, on every launch; AntiMicroX); in Dolphin, GCPad1 to GCPad4 in GCPadNew.ini with that mapping (4 = A, 1 = B, 5 = X, 2 = Y, 3 = Z, 6 = R, coin on the Triforce Coin) and each player's port enabled in Dolphin.ini; Flycast by device name (Dreamcast through SDL; arcade, Naomi and Atomiswave, with the panel's button N on the game's button N and coin on D; a hat D-pad becomes Flycast's hat button code and a trigger goes in its triggers list), and the same files in Fightcade's Flycast Dojo; Attract-Mode Plus, the joystick entries of input_map in attract.cfg: each player's directions, Select on button 1 and Back on button 2, plus its default shortcuts (Up + button 1 and so on) for player 1, replacing the old controller's entries (keyboard entries and other commands stay; it is written again every time Attract-Mode opens, so one installed after the mapping gets it too); Supermodel, Start, coin, directions and buttons 1 to 4 for players 1 and 2 in Supermodel.ini; Hypseus Singe, the [KEYBOARD] of ~/.hypseus/hypinput.ini (buttons 1 to 3 and skill 1 to 3, Start and coin for both players); and OpenBOR, in each player's keys of Saves/default.cfg and of each pak's .cfg (the s_savedata struct: 1 attack, 2 jump, 3 special, 4 to 6 the other attacks, coin on Esc). The map lives in /etc/fliperos/buttons.map, and a system update writes everything again. A trigger (an axis resting at one end, like an Xbox 360 controller's LT/RT) can only be pressed one way: if the capture started with it still held, it used to record the release, and the button then counted as pressed at rest in every emulator; the capture now ignores that release, and saving fixes the direction from the controller's resting position, also in a map saved before.

Buttons layout (games of 1 to 5 buttons)

Where the buttons of a game go on the panel, by how many buttons the game uses. Pick the number of buttons (1 to 5) and then, for button 1, button 2 and so on, its place on the panel (top row or bottom row; left, middle or right). The panel is drawn as you go, and the list shows the layout of each number:

3-button games   [1][2][3] / [ ][ ][ ]
4-button games   [1][2][ ] / [3][4][ ]      or      [1][3][ ] / [2][4][ ]

Without a layout, the game's button N is on the panel's button N (1 2 3 on top, 4 5 6 below); Default layout goes back to that. Six-button games always use the whole panel. It needs the Button mapping done first: the layout moves the buttons mapped there.

The choice is saved in /etc/fliperos/buttons.layout (one line per number of buttons: the panel position of button 1, of button 2...). How many buttons each game has comes from GroovyMAME's game list (-listxml) with the real counts of controls.xml on top (fliperos-romclean buttons writes the table /var/lib/fliperos/game-buttons.txt; the first time it reads the list, about a minute). The MAME 2010 core has a table of its own, game-buttons-mame2010.txt, from the game list of MAME 0.139: that version gives some games the generic button block of their board instead of the buttons the game has. Tekken 3 and the other System 12 games are buttons 1 2 4 5 of a block of 6 there, and 1 2 3 4 in today's MAME. Such a game (0.139 lists more buttons than the game has, and today's MAME lists only the game's) counts as its whole block, so no layout moves it: the punches stay on the top row and the kicks under them, as on the original panel. It applies where the panel's button N is the game's button N: in GroovyMAME, a <system> per game in the ctrlr fliperos.cfg (a clone follows its parent's); in RetroArch's MAME and MAME 2010 cores, a buttons-N.cfg next to buttons.cfg, which fliperos-kms-run adds to the config of a game with N buttons; in Supermodel (Model 3), the buttons of the game in its own section of Supermodel.ini ([ vf3 ]), which Supermodel adds to [ Global ] when the game opens — the fighting games (Punch, Kick, Guard, Escape), the Virtua Striker series (Short Pass, Long Pass, Shoot) and Spikeout (Shift, Beat, Charge, Jump), by the input type of each game in Games.xml; other settings you keep in that section stay; in SM2-Emu (Model 2), a game controller mapping of its own, /var/lib/fliperos/gamecontrollerdb-model2.txt and a gamecontrollerdb-model2-N.txt per layout, which fliperos-x11-run puts on top of the system's gamecontrollerdb.txt for that launch. SM2-Emu reads game buttons 1 to 4 from A B X Y and always takes RB as button 4, so the fight stick mapping of the other emulators put button 1 on the bottom row; its own mapping puts the panel's button N on the game's button N, layout or not. The Model 2 mapping is written with the panel connected (it goes by the device's GUID). The other emulators keep the Button mapping order: FBNeo arranges the buttons of each game by itself, and the others have one mapping for all games.

Calibrate GunCon 2

The screen turns white (the gun needs light where it aims) with a target in each corner; three shots at each (the median counts; a shot off the screen is rejected), and the calibrator computes the gun's real range on the tube — GunCon 2 is linear, so two known points give the edges. A shot at the center checks the result before saving /etc/fliperos/guncon2.conf, which the udev rule reapplies on every connection (evdev forgets the calibration).

Calibrate wheel, pedals or analog stick

The axes show live; turn the wheel from end to end and press each pedal all the way, then leave everything at rest. An axis that rests in the middle (wheel, analog stick) gets a symmetric range around the rest position — the center gives 0 and both sides reach the end — and a 1% dead zone; a pedal keeps the range seen. It goes to /etc/fliperos/calibration.conf (one line per axis) and udev reapplies it on every connection.

LPT joysticks

Joysticks and pads on the parallel port, through the kernel's own drivers: gamepad adapter (gamecon: NES, SNES, N64, PlayStation, DDR mat, Multisystem; up to 5 per port, on pins 10, 11, 12, 13 and 15), DB9 adapter (db9: Atari/Amiga/Master System, 3/5/6-button Genesis, Saturn, CD32; needs the port in EPP or ECP mode in the BIOS) and TurboGraFX interface (turbografx: up to 7 Multisystem joysticks). The adapter can't be detected: you say which one it is and what is connected to each input, and the setup writes /etc/modprobe.d/fliperos-lpt.conf (the module options, plus blacklist lp: the printer driver would claim the port, and the joystick drivers want it for themselves) and /etc/modules-load.d/fliperos-lpt.conf (loads at boot), loads the driver right away and shows the joysticks created. The kernel finds the port itself (parport_pc) when it exists — on the motherboard it often comes disabled in the BIOS (Super I/O > Parallel Port); without one, a PCI/PCIe parallel card. Parallel port joysticks also move the menus.

Setup > System > Quirks

Kernel fixes for USB devices that arrive wrong. It sits in System, not in Joysticks, because it is about any USB device. Two kinds, told apart by the code:

  • Controllers, guns, keyboards (the usbhid.quirks parameter): code 0xVENDOR:0xPRODUCT:0xFLAGS;
  • Disks and pen drives (the usb-storage.quirks parameter): code VENDOR:PRODUCT:LETTERS, without 0x, for example 152d:0578:u, where u turns UAS off for a SATA adapter that hangs or drops off.

Each quirk has a name you give it and a code, for example 0x16c0:0x05e1:0x40 for a dual Xin-Mo encoder to show up as two players. Choosing one from the list deletes it; USB devices connected now shows the vendor:product of each connected device. Useful flags: 0x40 one device per player (MULTI_INPUT, dual encoders), 0x8 no GET requests (NOGET, an encoder that hangs), 0x400 always poll (ALWAYS_POLL). The kernel reads at most 4 controller quirks (disk quirks have no such limit), and only at boot: the list lives in /etc/fliperos/quirks.conf, goes into the kernel line (/etc/default/fliperos-boot) and applies after a reboot. Done on the installation media, it goes along to the disk.

Controllers in SDL

AntiMicroX, PCSX2, Dolphin, Flycast and RetroArch use SDL2, which maps a controller outside its database automatically and incompletely: it leaves out the buttons without a counterpart on a modern controller (BTN_C, BTN_Z, extra buttons — in controller mode AntiMicroX doesn't even show them) and puts on the analog stick a digital D-pad that the device sends as axes from −1 to +1 (common in arcade encoders and V-USB projects), and then a game that moves with the D-pad doesn't move. fliperos-controllers (at boot and whenever a controller is connected, through udev) asks SDL itself how it sees each controller and, only for those with an automatic mapping or with no mapping and a digital D-pad, completes it: leftover buttons go to triggers, misc1 and paddles, and the digital axes become the D-pad. Controllers in SDL's database (Xbox, DualShock...) stay as they are; real analog ones (wheel, flight stick) too. The result goes to /var/lib/fliperos/gamecontrollerdb.txt (cumulative), pointed to by SDL_GAMECONTROLLERCONFIG_FILE in /etc/environment and in the launchers. Flycast builds its default mapping from SDL but doesn't understand a D-pad wired to a half axis; for those controllers it also gets mapping files (Dreamcast and arcade, in Flycast's own convention), only if there isn't one yet. It also ships with player 2 (port B) with a controller — its default leaves only player 1 — and with linear interpolation off (rend.LinearInterpolation = no in emu.cfg; the Linear Interpolation option in its settings): Flycast's default blurs the image when taking it to the tube's mode, and without the filter the pixels come out sharp. In an earlier emu.cfg, the option is only turned off if it isn't saved yet; once saved, the choice is yours. Texture Filtering comes as Force Nearest-Neighbor (rend.TextureFiltering = 1) for the same reason; Flycast saves its default (0) when it closes, so in an earlier emu.cfg it is changed once (marked in /var/lib/fliperos/flycast-texture-nearest) and after that the choice is yours. RetroArch is compiled with SDL2, so the sdl2 joypad driver shows in Drivers > Input next to udev, and the choice is kept (the system retroarch.cfg doesn't pin the joypad driver). The default is still udev, which shows in the menu the real number of each controller button; with sdl2 the same mappings as the other emulators apply, a controller without a profile falls back to the built-in "Standard Gamepad", and the numbers become SDL's (A = 0, B = 1...). SDL reads the mappings when it opens: a controller connected for the first time with RetroArch open only works correctly after reopening it. With udev, arcade panels and encoders were "not configured" (there's no factory profile for them); fliperos-controllers writes one in /opt/fliperos/retroarch/autoconfig/udev for every controller with a digital D-pad and no analog stick that no profile covers, with the buttons in panel order in the arcade cores' layout (FBNeo): 1 2 3 = Y X L (top row), 4 5 6 = B A R (bottom row) — the MAME cores read the RetroPad's Button 1-6 in another order (MAME, B A Y X L R; MAME 2010, A B X Y L R), and a core remap (config/retroarch-remaps, in ~/.config/retroarch/config/remaps/<core>; one saved from RetroArch's menu stays) takes the panel's button N to the game's Button N, as in GroovyMAME; on a panel with up to 9 buttons the 7th is Start and the 8th Select (coin); with 10 or more, 7 and 8 are L2/R2 and Select/Start move to 9 and 10, as on USB encoders. The profile carries the name and VID+PID (V-USB's VID+PID is the same across different projects) and isn't rewritten: one saved from the menu (Save Controller Profile) stays. RetroArch's menu always names the buttons after the RetroPad's (A, B, X, Y...); with an arcade core open, Quick Menu > Controls shows the game's names. An encoder whose D-pad is axes only and which the kernel merges into a single controller may need a quirk (Setup > System > Quirks).

Desktop and theme

LXDE desktop

GroovyArcade's LXDE: full lxde with openbox-lxde, panel at the bottom (menu, file manager, terminal, tasks, CPU, volume, tray, network, clock), no compositor, no DPMS or screensaver, Sans 10 font and the Video Setup orientation. The wallpaper is /usr/share/fliperos/wallpaper.jpg (config/wallpaper.jpg, a pixel-art city at night), stretched to the screen, which is 4:3 in every CRT mode. Everything in the Dracula theme (see below).

Terminal: Alacritty. lxterminal and xterm (the system's default terminal, with bitmap fonts the image doesn't even have) didn't draw Gum's borders; they were removed, and the terminal in the panel, the menu and FliperOS Setup is Alacritty, which always works in UTF-8 and draws borders and blocks by itself, without depending on the font. Dracula theme with the console palette (config/lxde/alacritty/alacritty.toml), DejaVu Sans Mono, non-blinking cursor (blinking redraws the screen, which flickers in interlaced modes). The font size doesn't follow the DPI from the CRT's EDID (it would give 50 DPI or less): fliperos-lxde exports WINIT_X11_SCALE_FACTOR=1. Since the lxde metapackage depends on lxterminal, the image installs its components one by one.

App Store: GNOME Software with the Flatpak plugin and Flathub as a source, in the menu (System Tools) and on the panel as App Store: Ubuntu packages and Flatpak apps (emulators, games, programs). Ubuntu 24.04's App Center depends on snap, which isn't in the image. No automatic update downloads and no welcome screen (config/fliperos-software.gschema.override): disk and network activity in the middle of a game hurts latency, for the same reason the apt timers are masked. Installing asks for the user's password (fliperos), through lxpolkit.

Disks on the desktop: every disk is mounted by itself, at boot and when it is plugged in (a second hard disk, a USB stick, a Windows partition), in /media/fliperos/<label>, and gets a hard disk icon on the desktop to open it. The mount is done by udisks2 as the fliperos user, so FAT, exFAT and NTFS disks belong to the user (NTFS through the kernel's ntfs3): a udev rule (config/99-fliperos-disks.rules) starts fliperos-automount@<device>.service for each file system, and config/fliperos-automount leaves out what is already mounted, swap, encrypted, LVM and RAID volumes, and system partitions (EFI, Windows recovery and reserved). A polkit rule (config/50-fliperos-udisks.rules) lets the user mount, unmount and eject internal disks without a password; formatting and partitioning still ask for it. The icons come from PCManFM (show_mounts=1) with gvfs, which also offers Unmount and Eject in the icon's menu. Nothing is mounted on the installation media, where a mounted disk couldn't be erased by the installer.

Browser and torrents: Falkon and Transmission ship in the image and appear in the menu (Internet) as Falkon (browser) and Transmission (torrent): fliperos-rootfs.sh copies each package's entry to /usr/local/share/applications, which takes precedence, with the name changed in every language.

Emulators in the menu (Games): RetroArch, GroovyMAME, Flycast, PCSX2, Supermodel, Dolphin, OpenBOR, Model 2 and, once downloaded through the Setup, Fightcade 2, with the projects' own icons. GroovyMAME, Model 2 (SM2-Emu) and Fightcade don't bring one into the image, so their official icons come from config/icons; Supermodel has no icon of its own and gets a FliperOS monogram. None of them runs inside the desktop's X — RetroArch and GroovyMAME use KMS, the others start an Xorg of their own in the mode from the modes table (Emulators) —, so the shortcut (fliperos-launch) saves the request and closes the desktop (with a confirmation if there are open windows), fliperos-session opens the emulator on the CRT and, when it closes, the desktop comes back. An emulator that wasn't built (--skip-*) doesn't show in the menu. Model 2 always does: it is not compiled into the image, and the first click clones and builds it (Emulators). Steam and Heroic (GOG), once installed through Setup > System > Extras, also live in Games, but open inside the desktop itself. GroovyArcade's tools also ship: AntiMicroX and QJoyPad (controller as keyboard/mouse), xterm, htop, evtest, joy2key, hwinfo, lshw, read-edid, i2c-tools. FliperOS Setup lives in System Tools (and on the panel), like gasetup.desktop. The terminal uses the Dracula colors. Leaving the desktop goes back to the menu.

Screen Resolution (Preferences): changes the desktop resolution on the fly. The list is the resolutions of the monitor chosen in Video Setup, plus Other resolution... (any WIDTHxHEIGHT@HZ). Switchres computes the mode within the ranges of /etc/switchres.ini (switchres --calc, the same calculation the emulators use) and xrandr applies it, adjusting the screen size; the window opens maximized and follows the change. Without confirmation within 15 seconds, the previous resolution comes back. Use every time the desktop starts saves the mode in ~/.config/fliperos/desktop-mode, which fliperos-lxde applies when the desktop opens; choosing the boot resolution forgets the saved one. Only the desktop changes: the console, the setup and the frontends keep the Video Setup resolution. With an LCD monitor, the list is the modes from its EDID.

Latency

What weighs most in the delay between pressing the button and the picture changing is the same on FliperOS and on GroovyArcade: the same kernel patches (the same video output on radeon/amdgpu), the same Switchres and GroovyMAME, and the CRT, which doesn't process the image. FliperOS applies the tweaks GA already makes and adds others, in two modes chosen in Setup > System > Latency (fliperos-setup/lib/latency.sh).

What each tweak does:

  • mitigations=off audit=0 — GA's default line. Turns off the kernel's Spectre/Meltdown protections, which cost the most on the older CPUs (without hardware fixes) typical of cabinets. It trades security for performance, acceptable on a machine that only runs games.
  • USB at 1000 Hz — a full-speed controller is polled every 8 ms (125 Hz); the average wait drops from ~4 ms to ~0.5 ms. It applies to whatever uses the usbhid driver (Zero Delay/Xin-Mo style encoders, I-PAC as a keyboard, trackball); Xbox controllers use xpad and don't change. If a controller misbehaves, usb_poll=default in /etc/fliperos/fliperos.conf and applying the mode again restores the original polling.
  • performance governor — frame delay counts on a fixed emulation time per frame; with the CPU raising its clock only after the load appears, that time varies. In the standard mode it only applies during the session (fliperos-session calls fliperos-setup --session-start/--session-end, like galauncher's cpu_governor.sh); in the low latency one, from boot (fliperos-latency.service).
  • preempt=full — FliperOS's 6.18 kernel is PREEMPT_DYNAMIC; with this parameter it interrupts any kernel work to run the emulator, which Ubuntu's old lowlatency kernel did. It reduces the variation (jitter) more than the average.
  • Frame delay (GroovyMAME's autoframedelay, RetroArch's video_frame_delay_auto) — the emulator waits part of the frame before emulating, and reads the controls closer to when the picture goes out: up to almost a frame (16.7 ms) less. The automatic one backs off by itself when the CPU can't keep up.
  • Preemptive frames — removes the game's own internal delay (1 frame), redoing the last frame only when the input changes (lighter than run-ahead, which redoes every frame). It needs a core with savestates; those without turn the feature off with a warning.
  • apt and man-db — Ubuntu's timers would run apt update and the man reindexing in the middle of a game. Updating lives in Setup > System > System Update, as in GA.

What was left out, and why: a PREEMPT_RT kernel (GA has a linux-rt), because preempt=full covers cabinet use and RT trades throughput for predictability, which is only worth it with measurements showing a gain; limiting the CPU C-states, because the gain would be microseconds when the CPU wakes up, against more power draw and heat; and RetroArch's video_hard_sync, because in KMS the 2-image swapchain already waits for each flip.

Compared with GA: on the same hardware, expect a tie in Standard mode (with an advantage of a few ms from USB at 1000 Hz) and up to 1 to 2 frames less in RetroArch games in Low latency mode. A hand-tuned GA gets to the same place; the difference is that FliperOS ships this way out of the box.

Measuring

The only reliable comparison is measuring, on the same cabinet, the same game and the same emulator version. Film the button and the screen together with a phone in 240 fps slow motion (~4 ms per video frame), 20 to 30 presses per configuration, and count the video frames between the button going down and the picture reacting.

Multiple monitors

Multiple monitors

Some arcade games were built for two or three monitors side by side or stacked: Darius and The Ninja Warriors (3 screens in a row), Punch-Out!! and Super Punch-Out!! (2 screens, one above the other), Ferrari F355 Challenge, Airline Pilots and Sega Strike Fighter (3 screens in a row, one NAOMI board per screen). FliperOS drives two arcade CRTs from the analog outputs of one video card, and up to three with a second video card or an active converter on a digital output (both experimental). When a game has 2 or more screens and the multi-screen mode is on, the emulator sends each screen to its own monitor. Every other game stays on monitor 1.

What you need

  • A video card with one analog output (VGA, DVI-I) for each monitor, supported by the 15 kHz kernel. The tested way is every monitor on the same card as monitor 1; a second card is experimental (below). Older Radeons with VGA + DVI-I (HD 5000, 6000 and 7000 series, like the HD 5450 or HD 6450) are the tested way to get 2 CRTs. These cards usually have two DACs, so even with VGA + 2 DVI-I outputs only two analog monitors work at the same time.
  • Check the DVI connector: only DVI-I (the 4 extra pins around the flat blade) carries analog VGA, and works with a passive DVI-to-VGA adapter. DVI-D is digital only. For example the Radeon R7 240 has VGA + DVI-D + HDMI: only one analog output.
  • You are not limited to one video card. The outputs can also come from more than one card: each card brings its own outputs (and its own DACs), so how many cards you can use only depends on how many free PCI/PCIe slots the computer has. Monitor 1's card draws the picture and the others only add outputs (see A second video card).
  • Monitors of the same type as monitor 1 (Setup > Video Setup > Monitor Type): they get the same video mode.
  • Monitor 1 already chosen by the output test (boot menu or Setup > Video Setup).

Digital outputs with an active converter (experimental)

An HDMI, DVI-D or DisplayPort output can drive a CRT through an active converter to VGA (one with a DAC chip inside; passive adapters don't work). The Setup lists these outputs as digital, active VGA converter, experimental. This is not tested yet on a cabinet: - A digital link can't go below about 25 MHz of pixel clock, so the extra monitor boots in the super resolution of the monitor's preset (video=HDMI-A-1:1280x480iSe at 15 kHz: the same lines and frequencies, with thinner pixels), and the Xorg configuration doesn't give it monitor 1's low-dotclock modeline. - Many cheap converters refuse non-standard modes, interlaced modes or low frequencies. If the monitor stays black, try another converter. - GroovyMAME's Switchres makes each game's mode for every screen with the card's own limits; on an AMD card these can go below the converter's minimum, and that screen goes black. Flycast's 3-screen games only use analog outputs for now.

A second video card (experimental)

The extra monitors don't have to be on monitor 1's card: they can be on another video card, or on several. If one card doesn't have enough analog outputs, add another; the only limit is how many free PCI/PCIe slots the computer has. The Setup lists the outputs of every card it finds, as card1:VGA-1 (AMD Radeon ...), and fliperos.conf saves them with the card in front (screens=VGA-1,DVI-I-1,card1:VGA-1). This is not tested yet on a cabinet: - Monitor 1's card draws everything. X opens on it (its BusID is in /etc/X11/xorg.conf.d/10-fliperos.conf) and adds the other card as a GPU screen (AutoAddGPU, AutoBindGPU): its outputs join the same desktop, and the picture is copied to them (reverse PRIME). In XRandR they get another name: VGA-1 of the first extra card is VGA-1-1, of the second VGA-1-2. fliperos-multiscreen xnames makes that translation for GroovyMAME and Flycast, and binds the card (xrandr --setprovideroutputsource) if X didn't. - The kernel's video= goes by connector name, on every card. video=VGA-1:640x480iSe sets the VGA-1 of both cards, so an extra monitor on a second card's VGA-1 gets monitor 1's mode, and an unused output with the same name on the other card is also forced on (with nothing plugged in, it does no harm). - Use a card the 15 kHz kernel supports with low dotclocks (AMD/ATI, radeon or amdgpu). On a card without them (Intel, NVIDIA) the monitor boots in the super resolution, and GroovyMAME's per-game modes may not reach it. - Programs in KMS (RetroArch, the frontends) only see monitor 1: the others are turned off while they run, as on one card.

Reports from anyone testing a converter or a second card are welcome in the issues.

Step by step

  1. Plug the monitors in. Monitor 1 is the one the boot menu and the output test found. Plug the others into free analog outputs (VGA, or DVI-I with a passive adapter), on the same card or on a second card. Turn them all on.
  2. Open the Setup: Setup > Video Setup > Multiple Monitors. The top shows monitor 1, monitors 2 and 3 (off) and the game screen order.
  3. Monitor 2: choose its output from the list (analog outputs of monitor 1's card first; digital outputs and other cards are marked experimental). The Setup asks if a monitor of the same type as monitor 1 is plugged in there. The first time it also prepares the GroovyMAME games with 2 or 3 screens (about a minute).
  4. Monitor 3, if you have one: same thing.
  5. Leave Video Setup: the Setup writes the kernel line (each extra monitor with its forced 15 kHz mode) and the Xorg configuration, and offers to reboot. The new monitors only light up after that.
  6. Identify Monitors: each one turns off for 3 seconds, in the game screen order. If the order is wrong (Darius' left screen on the right), fix it in Game Screen Order: left to right for Darius and F355 Challenge, top to bottom for Punch-Out!!.
  7. Play. Open a game with several screens from any frontend or the Games menu: GroovyMAME and Flycast send each screen to its monitor. Single-screen games, RetroArch and the frontends stay on monitor 1.
  8. Multi-Screen Games: Off puts every game back on monitor 1 without removing the monitors; Monitor 2/3: Off removes them (another reboot).

How it works

Boot. Each extra monitor gets the boot mode of monitor 1, forced on, in the kernel line (video=DVI-I-1:640x480iSe; with an EDID boot, the preset's mode; on a digital output, the preset's super resolution). Rotation (Monitor Orientation) applies to all of them. The fliperos.conf keeps the outputs in the game screen order (screens=DVI-I-1,VGA-1,DVI-I-2) and the multi-screen switch (multiscreen=off when off).

X. Each output of monitor 1's card has its own Monitor section in /etc/X11/xorg.conf.d/10-fliperos.conf, side by side in the game screen order, with monitor 1 as the primary screen: single-screen programs open there. Outputs of a second card come in as a GPU screen (see above).

KMS. RetroArch, Attract-Mode, EmulationStation and Pegasus open on the first connected output, which could be an extra monitor. So fliperos-kms-run turns the extra ones off (fliperos-setup --outputs main), and every X server (fliperos-x11-run, the desktop) turns them back on (--outputs all).

GroovyMAME. fliperos-mame-screens reads GroovyMAME's own -listxml and writes one .ini with numscreens per system with 2 or more screens in /etc/fliperos/mame/screens (limited to the number of monitors). This takes about a minute the first time and only runs again when the GroovyMAME binary or the number of monitors changes. The groovymame command adds that folder to the -inipath after the main one, so a user .ini in the main folder wins, and sends each window to its output (-screen0 screen1...). GroovyMAME's Switchres finds screenN in XRandR order and SDL counts the primary screen first; for both to agree the command makes the first output primary, and on the desktop restores the previous one when GroovyMAME closes. Switchres sets the mode of each game on every monitor.

Flycast. Flycast runs each NAOMI board of these games as its own process, with its own window (network:MultiboardSlaves=2): the main board where window:left says and the others one window width to each side. For the F355 Challenge, Airline Pilots and Sega Strike Fighter sets (f355, f355twin, f355twinp, f355twn2, alpilot, alpilotj, sstrkfgt, sstrkfgta), with 3 monitors and the switch on, fliperos-x11-run puts fliperos-multiscreen in front of Flycast. Inside the X server it gives the three outputs the mode Switchres made for monitor 1, side by side with no gap, and places the main board on the middle one; in full screen SDL puts each window on the monitor it falls on. The other boards are new Flycast processes that only read ~/.config/flycast/emu.cfg, so full screen and the stretch are saved there. The board BIOS (f355dlx, airlbios, naomi...) must be in a Flycast content folder, next to the game.

Problems

  • The Setup offers no output for monitor 2: no other output is free on any card. A DVI-D connector is not analog (see above).
  • An extra monitor stays black: check that you rebooted after choosing it, and use Identify Monitors. On a digital output, try another active converter. On a second card, xrandr --listproviders (in a terminal on the desktop) must show both cards, and xrandr must list its outputs (VGA-1-1...). The kernel line must have its video= (Setup > System > Debug mode shows /proc/cmdline).
  • The screens are swapped: change Game Screen Order.
  • A game opens on one monitor only: check that Multi-Screen Games is On. For GroovyMAME, /etc/fliperos/mame/screens/<game>.ini must exist (the Setup makes them when you add a monitor; groovymame remakes them otherwise). For Flycast, it needs 3 monitors.
  • Logs: /opt/fliperos/logs/groovymame.log and /opt/fliperos/logs/flycast.log (the fliperos-multiscreen lines show the layout used).

Hook Of The Reaper

Hook Of The Reaper is the current successor of MAMEHooker. It has no Linux version yet (its author plans one after the program is complete), so FliperOS runs the Windows program through Wine.

  • What is installed: 64-bit Wine and a virtual screen (xvfb) from apt, and the program from its own website (pinned version and hash) into ~/hotr. It gets its own Wine folder, separate from Fightcade's. Nothing of this is in the image.
  • Setting it up: on the desktop, Preferences > Hook Of The Reaper opens its window. Add your light guns and assign each one to a player, as its tutorial shows. The settings stay in ~/hotr/data.
  • Game files: it comes with its own files for over 200 gun games (~/hotr/defaultLG), which work with any gun it supports. MAMEHooker .ini files go in ~/hotr/ini/MAME. The folder is also at ~/config/hotr, shared on the network.
  • When it runs: it opens with each game of GroovyMAME, Flycast, Supermodel and SM2-Emu and closes with it. It runs on the virtual screen, so none of its windows or warnings appear over the game. Installing it again from the Extras updates the program and keeps your guns, players and .ini files.
  • USB HID guns and boards: the install lets the fliperos user open the USB HID devices (/dev/hidraw*, group input), which is how Wine reaches them. Serial boards already belong to the user's dialout group.

LEDSpicer

  • The install adds the packages for every board it supports, but doesn't turn the service on: LEDSpicer needs /etc/ledspicer.conf describing your boards and which LED is which button, as its wiki explains. Then sudo systemctl enable --now ledspicerd.
  • With the service running, FliperOS tells it which game opened (emitter LoadProfileByEmulator <game> arcade, or the console's folder for the others) and when it closed, from any frontend or emulator.

QMamehook

QMamehook is an open-source (LGPL-2.1) reader that runs natively on Linux, without Wine, and uses MAMEHooker's .ini files unchanged. It is small on purpose: it only writes to serial ports and only accepts the USB IDs of a few gun boards (OpenFIRE, GUN4IR, Blamcon; the RS3 Reaper after sudo modprobe usbserial vendor=0x0483 product=0x5750). It has no window and nothing to set up.

  • What is installed: its author publishes only the Windows program, so the install builds version 2.2 from source (pinned by hash) into ~/qmamehook. The compiler and Qt 6 come from apt; it takes about two minutes. Nothing of this is in the image.
  • Game files: MAMEHooker .ini files go in ~/qmamehook/ini, one per ROM set (sf2ce.ini). The folder is also at ~/config/qmamehook, shared on the network. For a game without a file, QMamehook writes one there listing the outputs the game sent, for you to fill in.
  • Players: the guns are numbered in the order of their USB product IDs, so the .ini writes to port 1 for the first gun, 2 for the second.
  • When it runs: it opens with each game of GroovyMAME, Flycast, Supermodel and SM2-Emu and closes with it. What it says (the guns it found, the file it loaded) is in /opt/fliperos/logs/qmamehook.log.
  • With Hook Of The Reaper also installed, only one of the two opens with the game, because a gun's serial port can't be shared: Hook Of The Reaper, unless /etc/fliperos/fliperos.conf has the line outputs_reader=qmamehook.

Adding a project to this page

A project fits here if its firmware or software is open source and it works on Linux without a closed driver. If you use one of these on your cabinet, or another one, open an issue in the repository saying which board, what the Setup showed in Setup > System > Quirks > USB devices connected now, and whether it needed a quirk: that is what turns a line of this page from "should work" into "tested".

15 kHz kernel

fliperos-kernel.sh downloads kernel.org 6.18.54, applies patches/kernel-15khz/6.18/ (D0023R, 01 to 09) on top of the .config of Ubuntu 24.04's own kernel and builds the .debs. No module signing and no debug info. Compiling takes hours, so the .debs stay in a cache keyed by version and patch hash (/output/kernel-cache/<key>/): the next build reuses them.

Switchres (v2.2.1, the same version as GroovyArcade's package) is compiled with grid and geometry, and at build time generates one EDID per monitor preset plus the two super resolution ones used by the boot's EDID entries (fliperos-rebuild-edids, GroovyArcade's rebuild_edids). The initramfs hook carries all of them into the initramfs.

Release

Every new ISO goes to GitHub Releases, with the version's changelog, like GroovyArcade's releases (substring/os): the text by area (system, packages, fliperos-setup, tools) and, next to the ISO, the package list.

  1. Write in CHANGELOG.md the ## VERSION section with what changed since the previous release (git log PREVIOUS_VERSION..HEAD lists the commits). A new ISO after a release needs a new version (FLIPEROS_VERSION in fliperos-mkiso.sh).
  2. Commit and push.
  3. Tag the version and push the tag (the name is the version, without "v"):

powershell git tag 0.10 git push origin 0.10

Pushing the tag triggers the GitHub Actions Release workflow (.github/workflows/release.yml). It checks that the tag is FLIPEROS_VERSION and that CHANGELOG.md has its section, builds the frontends repository (Dockerfile.packages) and generates the ISO with the same Dockerfile.fliperos as the local build. Then it publishes through tools/release-publish.sh, with the workflow's own token. The kernel and frontend .debs stay in the Actions cache, so they only recompile when the version, a patch or a recipe changes. Without the cache, the build takes about 3 hours on a 4-core runner (the limit is 6 h). If it fails, fliperos-mkiso.log is kept as a workflow artifact. Run by hand (Actions > Release > Run workflow), it generates the ISO of the current version and keeps it as an artifact for 3 days, without publishing. To redo a version that was just tagged, move the tag and push it again (git tag -f 0.10, git push -f origin 0.10): the build of the old tag that is still running is cancelled, and its release, if it was already published, is replaced.

To publish an ISO generated here, in the audit container:

docker build -t fliperos-vmtest -f tools/Dockerfile.vmtest tools
docker run -it --rm -v fliperos-gh:/root/.config/gh fliperos-vmtest gh auth login
docker run --rm -v "${PWD}:/w:ro" -v fliperos-gh:/root/.config/gh -w /w fliperos-vmtest bash tools/release-publish.sh /w/output/fliperos-0.10.iso

gh auth login is a one-time step: the login stays in the fliperos-gh volume.

tools/release-publish.sh only publishes what can go public:

  • the ISO goes through the audit (tools/verify-iso.sh), so it is the one from the current repository — an old ISO, generated before the latest commits, is rejected;
  • an ISO with a saved network (--wifi-ssid, --wifi-psk) is rejected: the password would go with it;
  • without the version's section in CHANGELOG.md, nothing is published;
  • an existing release isn't replaced without --replace (deletes and publishes again); the workflow passes it, because there a release only exists again when the tag was moved on purpose.

GitHub accepts up to 2 GiB per file, and the ISO stays below that. The build's slim_rootfs_chroot removes the -dev packages and the build tools, but keeps build-essential, dkms and the kernel headers, which the DKMS drivers use. No autoremove, because the Qt plugins and Vulkan come in through dlopen and don't show in ldd; the libraries ldd shows are marked as manually installed, and an ldd before and after fails the build if any is missing. Switchres opens libX11.so, libXrandr.so and libdrm.so by those unversioned names, which belong to the -dev packages: config/fliperos-dlopen-links scans the binaries in /usr/local and /opt for such names and links them in /usr/local/lib to the installed version, and tools/verify-iso.sh checks the three. The packages left with nothing depending on them once the -dev are gone (Qt SVG, sndio, hidapi, OpenAL) are marked as manually installed too, so an apt autoremove on the installed system doesn't take them. The apt cache and lists also go, as does the out-of-scope firmware (Mellanox, Marvell Prestera, Qualcomm, QLogic), through a dpkg path-exclude so an update doesn't bring it back. The GPU firmware stays, NVIDIA's included: nouveau needs it to accelerate the newer cards. The squashfs is xz. Wine, Steam and Heroic aren't in the image: Setup > System > Extras installs them (config/fliperos-extras). If the ISO goes over 2 GiB, it is split into 1900 MiB parts (fliperos-0.10.iso.001, .002...), and the release text gets the instructions to join them (copy /b on Windows, cat on Linux). Also published: fliperos-0.10.sha256 (the whole ISO and each part) and fliperos-0.10-pkglist.txt (package, version and architecture of what's in the image). --prerelease marks it as a pre-release; --to FOLDER only prepares the files and the text (notes.md), without touching GitHub; --notes VERSION shows the version's text.

Launch commands and wrappers

Nothing on FliperOS opens a frontend or an emulator by calling its binary directly. Between the boot and the game there is a short chain of scripts, each one with a single job. All of them are in /opt/fliperos/bin, except fliperos-menu and groovymame, which are in /usr/local/bin so they can be typed in the shell.

Command Called by What it does
fliperos-tty1 ~/.zprofile, at the tty1 login The boot flow: first boot or update notice, then the default launcher, then the menu loop
fliperos-menu You, in the shell Back to the menu: runs fliperos-tty1 --menu and, on leaving, shows the MOTD again
fliperos-session [name] fliperos-tty1 Opens one launcher from the sessions table and returns when it closes
fliperos-kms-run program [arguments] fliperos-session, frontends, you Runs a program straight on KMS/DRM, without Xorg
fliperos-x11-run [--mode WxH@HZ] program [arguments] Frontends, fliperos-launch, you Runs a program in an Xorg of its own, in a video mode made by Switchres
fliperos-x11-client fliperos-x11-run, through xinit Inside that Xorg: window manager, then Switchres switches the mode and launches the program
fliperos-launch name The Games menu of the desktop Closes the desktop, opens the emulator, brings the desktop back
fliperos-escquit PID fliperos-session, fliperos-fightcade Esc closes the frontend when no game is open
fliperos-powerkey triggerhappy, on the power button Closes the open game; with no game open, powers off

From the boot to the launcher

fliperos-tty1 is the only entry point. At boot it runs fliperos-session with no argument, which opens the launcher chosen in Setup > Games > Frontend (/etc/fliperos/session); when that closes, it enters the menu loop. In the loop the Setup never opens anything itself: Start frontend and Start desktop write the launcher's name to /run/fliperos/launch and exit with code 20, and fliperos-tty1 then calls fliperos-session <name> from the login shell, where tty1 is the controlling terminal (Installed system explains why). fliperos-menu is the same loop without the boot part.

fliperos-session looks the name up in /etc/fliperos/sessions.conf (and in sessions.custom.conf, the frontends you registered), one launcher per line as name|backend|binary|package|description. The backend decides how it opens:

  • kms: fliperos-kms-run <binary>. For the frontends (Attract-Mode Plus, ES-DE, RetroFE, Pegasus and your own) it also starts fliperos-escquit and marks the process with FLIPEROS_POWERKEY=frontend.
  • x: xinit <binary> on the same VT. Only the LXDE desktop uses it.
  • none: opens nothing; the boot goes straight to the menu.

Around the launcher it sets the CPU governor to performance and restores it afterwards (fliperos-setup --session-start and --session-end).

The two wrappers: KMS and X11

Every emulator is opened through one of them, and both refuse to run inside an X session (DISPLAY set): they need the console. Which one to use for each emulator is in Adding games.

fliperos-kms-run is for what draws on KMS by itself: RetroArch and the KMS frontends. The video mode is the one from the boot, and RetroArch switches it per game on its own. Before the program starts, the wrapper:

  • tells the Pixelcade marquee and the game outputs (fliperos-pixelcade launch, fliperos-outputs launch) which game is opening;
  • for RetroArch, stacks the system configuration on top of the user's with --appendconfig (the FliperOS retroarch.cfg, the gl fallback for cards without OpenGL 3.2, the Button mapping, DOFLinx and the versus score);
  • turns off the extra monitors, leaving only the main one;
  • exports the console drivers: SDL_VIDEODRIVER=kmsdrm, SDL_AUDIODRIVER=alsa and Qt's eglfs;
  • gives the program the session's terminal as its input when a frontend handed it /dev/null;
  • sends the program's output to /opt/fliperos/logs/<program>.log (to the screen in Debug mode).

fliperos-x11-run is for what needs X: Flycast, PCSX2, Dolphin, Supermodel, Model 2, OpenBOR, Hypseus. It starts an Xorg just for that program and closes it when the program exits. The mode comes from --mode, or from the program's line in /etc/fliperos/emulator-modes.conf (one column for a 15 kHz monitor, another for 25/31 kHz and LCD), or is 320x240@60. It also adds each emulator's own full-screen and stretch options, turns the extra monitors back on, exports SDL_VIDEODRIVER=x11, and writes the same log. The Xorg's client is fliperos-x11-client: it starts Openbox (which fits the window to the screen at every mode switch) and hands over to switchres WIDTH HEIGHT HZ --switch --launch "<program>", which creates the modeline for the monitor in /etc/switchres.ini, switches to it and runs the program. More in Emulators.

Commands that wrap themselves

Two launchers are kms in the sessions table but need X. Their own command notices it is on the console and opens itself again through fliperos-x11-run:

  • groovymame (config/fliperos-groovymame): the real binary is /usr/local/libexec/groovymame. Commands with no screen (-listxml, -verifyroms...) run directly.
  • /opt/fliperos/fightcade/fightcade (a link to fliperos-fightcade, see Fightcade 2).

So choosing Fightcade in the menu runs this chain, each line starting the next:

fliperos-tty1                                    the menu loop
└─ fliperos-session fightcade                    the row in sessions.conf, CPU governor
   └─ fliperos-kms-run /opt/fliperos/fightcade/fightcade      log, Pixelcade, outputs
      └─ fliperos-fightcade                      no DISPLAY: opens itself in X
         └─ fliperos-x11-run /opt/fliperos/fightcade/fightcade    mode from the table, xinit
            └─ fliperos-x11-client               Openbox, Switchres switches the mode
               └─ fliperos-fightcade             DISPLAY set: Wine prefix, emulator settings
                  └─ Fightcade2.sh               the Fightcade client

From the desktop

The desktop is itself an X session, and the wrappers don't run inside one. fliperos-launch <name> (the items in Games, see Desktop and theme) writes the full command, already with its wrapper, to $XDG_RUNTIME_DIR/fliperos-next and closes LXDE. fliperos-session, which was waiting for the desktop's X to end, runs that command with FLIPEROS_POWERKEY=game and then opens the desktop again. It only accepts a request that starts with fliperos-kms-run or fliperos-x11-run. fliperos-launch --mode 640x240@60 program does the same for any program.

Other components

Splash. Plymouth. The default fliperos-text theme is text only, in the style of a modern terminal: the name scrambles into place and then waves through a gradient, a prompt types the subtitle, a gradient progress bar with a moving shine, the last systemd units with a spinner (SetUpdateStatusFunction) and faint falling characters. It draws everything with JetBrains Mono (OFL, config/fonts), which goes to /usr/share/fonts/truetype/fliperos and, through the fliperos-fonts initramfs hook, into the initramfs: the Ubuntu plymouth hook only copies the Ubuntu font. fliperos is a full 640x480 image scaled to the boot mode (640x480, 640x240, 320x240...) with an animated bar. Three themes from Gnome-look come too: cogwheel (by DUKE93, GPL 3), into-it and windoze95, adapted to scale to the window, with the pixel twice as tall in the double-width modes (640x240), and Windoze 95's art reduced to 640x480. All of them live in config/splashscreen and go to /usr/share/plymouth/themes and to ~/splashscreen, where Setup > System > Splash screen also lists the user's themes (more on Gnome-look). Setting one (lib/splash.sh) copies its folder with ImageDir and ScriptFile rewritten, points the default.plymouth alternative at it and runs update-initramfs (the initramfs hook takes the new initrd to the ESP); the preview runs plymouthd with plymouth.splash=NAME. tools/cabinet-update.sh rebuilds the initramfs when the boot theme's files change. The build can also use evangelion (202 frames, with a seizure risk declared by the author and no license in the package) or none.

Network. Samba (\\<ip>\FliperOS → /opt/fliperos, and \\<ip>\roms, bios, media and config → the folders of the same name in ~, writable only with the fliperos user; config/smb.conf), SSH/SFTP and Avahi (fliperos.local). Default user fliperos with password fliperos, like GroovyArcade's user arcade with password arcade (see Installed system). NetworkManager handles Wi-Fi and wired (the empty 10-globally-managed-devices.conf overrides Ubuntu's, which only managed Wi-Fi) and writes /etc/resolv.conf itself with the DNS from DHCP (90-fliperos-dns.conf: Ubuntu would hand DNS to systemd-resolved, which isn't in the image). The clock comes from the network (systemd-timesyncd). On the first test on the cabinet all three were missing: resolv.conf was the build container's, the BIOS clock was two months behind, and apt didn't work (neither System Update nor frontends).

Diagnostics. sudo fliperos-video-check --json reads the active mode of each output (current CRTC through libdrm, read-only): connector, resolution, kHz, Hz, interlaced.
